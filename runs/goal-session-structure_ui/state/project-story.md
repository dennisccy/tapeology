# Project story so far

Tapeology is a trading-research tool that reads live market tape, measures trading ideas honestly, and is now opening up its research work, piece by piece, to a browser view anyone can see.

## How it has grown

Tapeology began as a tool that reads live stock-trade data and tells a trader, moment to moment, whether buyers or sellers are winning the tape, with a journal for trading ideas and replay studies for practice. The next chapter turned that skill into an honest profit-measuring machine, with a Performance page showing whether a strategy would have made or lost money, always next to a fair comparison. The chapter after that taught Tapeology to find real support-and-resistance price levels in stored history, group them into graded zones, and build a second, zone-aware trading approach — an honest head-to-head test found no proof yet that it beat the original, so the original kept its title. Until now, none of this deeper research could be seen by looking at the app itself.

This current chapter set out to change that. It opened with an honest stock-take confirming nothing had broken and that the promised new screen didn't exist yet, then delivered the first real piece: a new "Structure" tab, reachable from the main navigation, where picking a stock and a point in time draws that symbol's key price levels on a chart and groups them into zones graded by strength, A being strongest. Testing was thorough — of fifteen checks, fourteen passed on the first try. The one that didn't mattered: in one narrow situation the chart could go blank instead of honestly saying there was nothing to show, exactly the silent gap this project won't allow. It was found, fixed the same day, and confirmed working again with a fresh screenshot; one more round of independent testing is planned before full sign-off. Everything that already worked — the tape reading, the journal, the replay studies, the Performance scorecard — still behaves exactly as before.

Next up: showing which trading strategies exist side by side, and which one currently holds the title, as a second section on this same screen.

## What it can do today

The product lets users type in a stock ticker to watch live trade-by-trade tape reading, write trading ideas into a journal, run replay studies, and check an honest profit-and-loss scorecard on a Performance page. It also now has a Structure tab where picking a stock and a point in time draws that symbol's key price levels on a chart and groups them into strength-graded zones — the first piece of the app's deeper research made visible in the browser, with the strategy lineup and comparison still to come.
