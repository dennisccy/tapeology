# Project story so far

Tapeology is a trading-research tool that reads live market tape, measures trading ideas honestly, and is now opening up its research work, piece by piece, to a browser view anyone can see.

## How it has grown

Tapeology began as a tool that reads live stock-trade data and tells a trader, moment to moment, whether buyers or sellers are winning the tape, with a journal for writing down trading ideas and replay studies for practicing against past market action. The next chapter turned that trade-reading skill into an honest profit-measuring machine, complete with a Performance page in the app showing whether a strategy would have made or lost money — always next to a fair random-guessing comparison, and always saying plainly when there wasn't yet enough evidence to trust a result.

The chapter after that taught Tapeology to find genuine support-and-resistance price levels in stored market history, group them into zones graded by how convincing they are, and build a second, zone-aware trading approach that only acts when the live tape agrees with a strong zone. An honest head-to-head test against the original approach — trained on some history, tested on history it had never seen — found no proof yet that the new approach was better, so the original kept its title, exactly as an honest test should behave. All of that work, though, only ever lived in the product's internal tools and its data connection — nobody could see a price level or a zone by looking at the app itself.

This new chapter starts by taking stock, honestly, before building anything: the team re-ran every existing check and confirmed nothing had broken, and confirmed — by actually trying to reach it — that the promised new screen for seeing those levels and zones genuinely does not exist in the app yet. Three of the four things this chapter must deliver — seeing levels and zones on a chart, seeing the lineup of trading approaches and which one is winning, seeing the honest comparison between the new zone-aware approach and the original — are confirmed not yet on screen, while the fourth, proving the rest of the app still works exactly as before, is already true. With that honest starting line drawn, the next step is to build the first real piece: a new screen that finally puts those price levels and zones on a chart.

## What it can do today

The product lets users type in a stock ticker and watch live trade-by-trade tape reading, write trading ideas into a journal, run replay studies, and view an honest backtest and profit-and-loss scorecard on a Performance page. Behind the scenes, it has also already worked out real support-and-resistance price levels and graded zones, and a second experimental trading approach compared honestly against the original — but none of that is visible on any screen yet.
