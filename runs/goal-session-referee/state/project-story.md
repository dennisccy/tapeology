# Project story so far

Tapeology is a research app with three screens — a live-tape Cockpit, a Structure page that maps where a stock tends to bounce or stall, and a Desk that scans for chart setups and logs every signal it sees, honestly, without ever placing a real trade.

## How it has grown

This chapter, "The Referee," is teaching the product to check its own homework — so a pattern noticed by eye can be told apart from a shape imagined in noise. It opened by confirming nothing new existed yet and everything old still worked, then did quiet groundwork: an evidence counter, a shared record shape so every logged signal and recorded trade compares the same way, and the actual judge — a statistics engine deciding whether a pattern is real, tightened after the team caught it once being overconfident in its own numbers.

Next came a way to compare every recorded signal against fair "nothing special happened" moments from the same stock, measured identically to the real signal. Then came a permanent notebook for research questions: a question can be written down before anyone checks whether it's true, its start date stamped automatically and never editable afterward — a real backdating hole that would have let old data count as fresh proof was found and closed before anything was ever saved for real.

This round built the actual judge for those written-down questions: a question can now be measured against its fair comparisons and come back with one permanent verdict — real evidence, no evidence, or too shaky to trust — that no later check can change. Double-checking this new machinery, the team found two more soft spots: a failed maths self-check was still letting the system write a falsely confident permanent answer (only what a person sees was correctly held back), and a damaged question record could vanish from the list instead of showing a warning. Neither has affected anyone yet — no real question has been written down for keeps. The old three screens still work exactly as before, re-confirmed again this round with a fresh look.

Next up: the first screen a person can actually use for this — picking a candidate question from a shortlist and registering it for real — plus fixing those two soft spots.

## What it can do today

The product lets users watch the live tape on the Cockpit, look up a stock's price map on the Structure page, and scan chart setups on the Desk — the same three screens as before. The Referee chapter's fact-checking work is now six rounds deep — a statistics engine, fair comparison moments, a research-question notebook, and the judge that measures a question and records its verdict — but none of it has a page of its own yet, so there's nothing new to click.
