# Project story so far

Tapeology is a research tool for reading a stock's price action right now and mapping its key price levels — an honest study instrument, never a trading system.

## How it has grown

The product started as a two-page instrument (a live Cockpit and a Structure page for support and resistance) before this chapter, "The Desk," added a third page: a daily ~100-stock screen with scan history, a chart drill-in, and a read-only Claude connection, plus honesty fixes like a top-up run log and a coverage-repair button. Rows then gained a price range/close and an "opposite wall" column, corrected once after it first named the wrong wall on a couple of stocks.

Several rounds turned from building to proving: recapturing a missing picture, fixing a broken recording script, then recording a guided walkthrough video whose every spoken number was checked against the saved data. One thing stayed undone — a small hover pop-up the normal screenshot tool could never capture — and the project paused for three rounds waiting on the owner. The owner then approved a special capture method that could prove the pop-up wasn't faked, the picture was taken, and the chapter's original fourteen capabilities were complete.

Rather than stop, the project kept improving on its own initiative. It first noticed that two rows could read identically on every column while one wall was built from a single price touch and another from hundreds, and closed that gap: every ranked row gained a note on how many price levels its wall is built from, how those levels split across timeframes, and whether the wall sits at a round number — proven row by row against the product's trusted computation for all one hundred ranked rows, zero mismatches. That left the table wide enough that its two newest columns needed a sideways scroll to see. This latest round fixed exactly that: the whole ranked table was redesigned to fit a normal screen with no sideways scrolling at all, and each row is now about half its old height, so far more of them are visible at once without extra scrolling either. Nothing about the numbers shown changed — only how they are laid out. Along the way, a review caught that two of the label words removed from the redesign were secretly relied on by two of the saved proof recordings, so those two words were put back before anything shipped. The chapter is once again nearly done: the layout fix works and passes every check that ran, but two routine double-checks ran out of time this round and a short recorded video of the new layout is still owed, so confirmation is being held one more short round.

## What it can do today

The product lets users run a simulated tape-reading session with live moving price bars, open a Structure page showing a stock's support and resistance on a real chart, and open a Desk page that screens about 100 stocks and ranks them. Each row shows how much history backs it up, its wall's price range and close, the nearest wall on the other side of price, how many price levels built that wall, its round-number status, and its timeframe breakdown — all fitting on one normal-sized screen with no sideways scrolling needed. Users can also hover a row for the wall-grade detail, repair the Desk's coverage badges, browse past scans, jump from a saved scan into the matching Structure chart, and read Desk data through a connected Claude conversation.
