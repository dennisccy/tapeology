# Project story so far

Tapeology is a research tool for reading a stock's price action right now and mapping its key price levels — an honest study instrument, never a trading system.

## How it has grown

Before this chapter, the product was a two-page instrument — a live Cockpit and a Structure page for support and resistance. This chapter, "The Desk," added a third page, a daily ~100-stock screen, which grew a memory of past scans, a chart drill-in, and a read-only Claude connection, then closed all seven of its original promises with real proof.

A routine review then found one more honest gap: a ranked stock's distance-to-wall could be measured from a same-day price or an eleven-day-old one, with no way to tell which. The team closed it — every row now names the date its measurement came from and how old that reading is — and proved the exact freshness spread the plan required after retaking one photograph on a disposable copy of the data.

With all eight of that chapter's promises proven, a routine check found one more real gap: refreshing a stock's price history used to lose its outcome the moment the next refresh started, so nobody could see what an earlier run had done. This round fixed that — a new panel on the Desk page now lists every refresh run ever completed: what it tried, reused, freshly fetched, and, for anything that failed, the plain reason why, plus an honest count of anything a stopped run never reached. The team proved it by re-running the checks itself on a disposable copy of the data. Still missing: a short recorded guide that shows a finished run, not just today's empty starting screen — the next, small piece of work.

## What it can do today

The product lets users run a simulated tape-reading session that settles into a read like "Buyer Control," with live moving price bars; open a Structure page showing a stock's support and resistance levels on a real chart, with examples of past price touches; and open a Desk page that screens about 100 stocks, refreshes their price history, ranks them, and shows how old each row's reading is. Users can jump from any past scan into the Structure chart for that stock and date, a connected Claude conversation can read the Desk's data directly, and every price-refresh run is now kept on permanent record for later review.
