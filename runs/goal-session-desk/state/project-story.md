# Project story so far

Tapeology is a research tool for reading how a stock's price is behaving right now and understanding its key price levels — an honest instrument for study, never a trading system.

## How it has grown

Before this chapter began, the product was already a two-page instrument: a live Cockpit for reading a stock's price action, and a Structure page for mapping a chosen stock's key support and resistance levels. This chapter, nicknamed "The Desk," set out to add a third page: a daily screening desk that scans roughly 100 well-known stocks and points the operator at which ones have interesting price levels worth a look today. Early rounds taught the system to fetch and save the company list, check and fill in each company's price history, and rank companies by how close they sit to a key price level — then put that ranking on screen as a new "Desk" page with a "Run Screen" button and a "Top-up" button, catching and fixing a real bug along the way where a blank-priced entry from the price feed could quietly break the price chart.

A later round finished proving the Desk page properly, taking every required photograph of it working, including one of a scan running live with a repeat click refused. The round after that gave the Desk a memory: an operator can now click any past scan and see exactly what was recorded that day, and every row — even a skipped one — can be clicked to jump straight into the Structure chart for that stock and date, already loaded.

This latest round closed out two of the three remaining loose ends. Claude, through the project's tool interface, can now read the Desk's company list and scan results directly — the same data the Desk page already shows — bringing its readable toolset to 17. A small bug from the previous round, where hovering over a scan row had stopped showing its full detail, is fixed: the detail is back, reachable from anywhere in the row, and clicking still works exactly as before. The team also finally took the last missing photographs proving the rest of the product — the simulated trading view, the price-level case study, and the report panel — still works correctly. But the chapter cannot close yet: a data-repair fix made a few rounds ago touched two parts of the product that were supposed to stay untouched this chapter, and only the owner can say whether that fix may stay, must be undone, or the rule should be reworded. The automation has paused there, waiting for that one answer.

## What it can do today

The product lets users run a simulated tape-reading session and watch it settle into a read such as "Buyer Control," with live moving price bars. It lets them open a Structure page to pick a stock and date and see its key support and resistance levels mapped over a real price chart, and open a case study for a past price touch. It lets them open a Desk page that scans about 100 well-known stocks, run a fresh ranking on demand, refresh the underlying price history with one click, and see the fully ranked result with an honest note on every stock that couldn't be ranked and why. It lets them revisit any past scan exactly as it was recorded, and jump from any scan row straight into the Structure chart for that stock and date, already loaded. Through a connected Claude conversation, Claude can now read the Desk's company list and scan results directly too.
