# Project story so far

Tapeology is a research tool for reading a stock's price action right now and mapping its key price levels — an honest study instrument, never a trading system.

## How it has grown

The product started as a two-page instrument (a live Cockpit and a Structure page for support and resistance) before this chapter, "The Desk," added a third page: a daily ~100-stock screen with scan history, a chart drill-in, and a read-only Claude connection, plus honesty fixes like a top-up run log and a coverage-repair button. Rows then gained a price range/close and an "opposite wall" column, corrected once after it first named the wrong wall on a couple of stocks.

Several recent rounds turned from building to proving: recapturing a missing picture, fixing a broken recording script, then recording a guided walkthrough video whose every spoken number was checked against the saved data. One thing stayed undone — a small hover pop-up the normal screenshot tool could never capture — and the project paused for three rounds waiting on the owner. The owner then approved a special capture method that could prove the pop-up wasn't faked, the picture was taken, and the chapter's original fourteen capabilities were complete.

Rather than stop, the project kept improving on its own initiative: it measured the briefing against itself and found that two rows could read identically on every column while one wall was built from a single price touch and another from hundreds. This latest round closed that gap — every ranked row now also shows how many price levels its wall is built from, how those levels split across timeframes, and whether the wall sits at a round number, proven row by row against the product's own trusted computation for all one hundred ranked rows with zero mismatches. Nothing existing changed. The chapter is complete again and once more waiting on the owner's confirmation to close, with two small non-blocking notes left for later (a wide table that now needs sideways scrolling to see its newest columns, and a stray wording issue in one internal check).

## What it can do today

The product lets users run a simulated tape-reading session with live moving price bars, open a Structure page showing a stock's support and resistance on a real chart, and open a Desk page that screens about 100 stocks and ranks them — each row showing how much history backs it up, its wall's price range and close, the nearest wall on the other side of price, and now how many price levels built that wall, its round-number status, and its timeframe breakdown. Users can hover a row for the wall-grade detail, repair the Desk's coverage badges, browse past scans, jump from a saved scan into the matching Structure chart, and read Desk data through a connected Claude conversation.
