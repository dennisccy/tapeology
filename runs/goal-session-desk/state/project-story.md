# Project story so far

Tapeology is a research tool for reading a stock's price action right now and mapping its key price levels — an honest study instrument, never a trading system.

## How it has grown

The product began as a two-page instrument — a live Cockpit and a Structure page for support and resistance — then gained a third page, "The Desk," with a daily ~100-stock screen, scan history, a chart drill-in, a top-up log, a coverage-repair button, and a read-only Claude connection, with early rounds polishing its ranked-row details and fitting its table to one screen with no sideways scrolling. The Desk's "top up my data" button was then made to say honestly what it actually asked the data supplier for and got back, proven number-for-number against a real run, and the team confirmed the Desk finished with seventeen abilities in place.

One more idea then arrived: give the Desk's own daily scan a permanent run history, like its two smaller siblings already had. The team built it — every scan attempt is now recorded, whether it fully computes, reuses a prior result, is cancelled, or fails, and a repeat scan on unchanged data answers in a fraction of a second instead of redoing a full check. The team called the Desk finished a fourth time, but the confirmation was turned back on one ground: nobody had photographed the new scan-history panel's honest "nothing recorded yet" message. A following round closed that gap and photographed the empty state, though it ran short on time and left two small wording fixes, a few tests, and a stray housekeeping slip for later. The next round finished that unfinished business: a repeat scan's summary box stopped showing a false-looking warning next to its own honest "nothing to do" message, a scan that fails before reaching any company stopped wrongly blaming the first name on the list, the stray files were put right, and the walkthrough video was re-recorded properly. Every one of the Desk's eighteen abilities was checked and the team called it finished again.

The most recent round added a genuinely new idea on top of that confirmed state: after a top-up, the Desk now tells the operator the actual date each stock's price history reaches — not just the window it asked the data supplier for. The team built it, then triggered one real top-up covering all 404 stock/timeframe pairs and checked every single one, one by one, against the price library itself, finding zero disagreements. The new line and list appeared correctly on screen in one screenshot, right beside the rest of the already-working page. All nineteen of the Desk's abilities are now checked and working, nothing broke, and nothing of the owner's existing data was disturbed — only 404 brand-new price records were added. The team has again called the Desk finished, with a short walkthrough video of this newest feature and two saved test scripts left as small, non-blocking tidying for later. A second, independent read-through is the only thing still standing between here and a confirmed finish.

## What it can do today

The product lets users run a simulated tape-reading session with live moving price bars on the Cockpit, open a Structure page showing a stock's support and resistance on a real chart, and open a Desk page that screens about 100 stocks and ranks them — each row showing its history depth, price range and close, opposite wall, and level makeup, all fitting one screen with no sideways scrolling. Users can hover a row for wall-grade detail, repair the Desk's coverage badges, browse past scans and jump into the matching Structure chart, read Desk data through a connected Claude conversation, top up stored price history while seeing an honest account of what each stock's fetch asked for and got back — including the actual date each stock's history now reaches and which stocks are still behind — and see a permanent, truthful record of every scan ever run, including reused, cancelled, or failed ones, with a repeat scan on unchanged data answering almost instantly.
