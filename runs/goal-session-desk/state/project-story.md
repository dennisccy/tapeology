# Project story so far

Tapeology is a research tool for reading a stock's price action right now and mapping its key price levels — an honest study instrument, never a trading system.

## How it has grown

Before this chapter, the product was a two-page instrument — a live Cockpit and a Structure page for support and resistance; this chapter, "The Desk," added a third page, a daily ~100-stock screen, built in stages and fixing a bad-price bug along the way.

Later rounds gave the Desk a memory — clicking a past scan shows what was recorded, and any row jumps into the Structure chart already loaded — and let a connected Claude conversation read the Desk's data directly. The chapter then stalled for one round on the owner's written word about an earlier data-repair fix; once it arrived, naming the exact files allowed to change, the team proved nothing else had shifted and took the last missing photograph, closing all seven original promises with real proof.

A routine review then found one more honest gap: a ranked stock's distance-to-wall could be measured from a same-day price or an eleven-day-old one, with no way to tell which. The team closed it — every row now names the date its measurement came from and how old that reading is — but the first proof photo showed three days beside fourteen instead of the sharper spread the plan required.

This round retook that photograph on a disposable copy of the data, leaving the owner's real records untouched. It now shows a two-day-old reading beside a twelve-day-old one, exactly as required, with every number checked row by row against the recorded file. All eight of this chapter's promises now have real, opened proof, nothing that used to work has broken, and the chapter is complete — the team is pausing for the owner to confirm the finish.

## What it can do today

The product lets users run a simulated tape-reading session that settles into a read like "Buyer Control," with live moving price bars; open a Structure page to see a stock's support and resistance levels on a real chart, with case studies of past price touches; and open a Desk page that screens about 100 stocks, refreshes price history, runs a fresh ranking, and shows how old each row's price reading is, with an honest note on anything unrankable. Users can jump from any past scan's row into the Structure chart for that stock and date, and a connected Claude conversation can read the Desk's data directly too.
