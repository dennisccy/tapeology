# Project story so far

Tapeology is a research tool for reading a stock's price action right now and mapping its key price levels — an honest study instrument, never a trading system.

## How it has grown

The product started as a two-page instrument (a live Cockpit and a Structure page for support and resistance). This chapter, "The Desk," added a third page: a daily ~100-stock screen with scan history, a chart drill-in, and a read-only Claude connection, plus honesty fixes like a top-up run log and a coverage-repair button. Rows then gained a price range/close and an "opposite wall" column, corrected once after it first named the wrong wall on a couple of stocks.

Several rounds turned from building to proving: recapturing a missing picture, fixing a broken recording script, recording a guided walkthrough video whose every spoken number was checked against the saved data. One thing stayed undone — a small hover pop-up the normal screenshot tool could never capture — and the project paused for three rounds waiting on the owner, who then approved a special capture method proving the pop-up wasn't faked; the picture was taken and the chapter's original fourteen capabilities were complete.

Rather than stop, the project kept improving on its own initiative. It noticed two rows could read identically on every column while one wall was built from a single price touch and another from hundreds, and closed that gap: every ranked row gained a note on how many price levels its wall is built from, the timeframe split, and whether it sits at a round number — proven row by row against the product's own trusted math for all one hundred ranked rows, zero mismatches. That left the table too wide for its two newest columns to be seen without a sideways scroll. The next round fixed exactly that: the whole ranked table was redesigned to fit a normal screen with no sideways scrolling, each row about half its old height, with a review catching (and restoring) two label words the redesign had accidentally broken in two saved proof recordings. That round finished the layout fix but ran out of time to record a video of it and to double-check two older items, so confirmation waited one more short round.

This latest round closed every remaining gap with no changes to the product itself. It recorded the still-missing guided video, and this time the video's own frames genuinely show the two right-hand columns it talks about. It re-confirmed the page's built-in Claude connection still offers exactly the right seventeen tools, and re-checked the wall-composition numbers against the saved data with zero mismatches. It also recovered a screenshot a previous round had claimed to save but never actually had. Every one of the Desk's sixteen capabilities is now built, shown and proven, with nothing of the owner's saved data touched. The project is judged finished, pending the owner's own confirmation, with only a few optional cosmetic notes left on the video's presentation.

## What it can do today

The product lets users run a simulated tape-reading session with live moving price bars on the Cockpit, open a Structure page showing a stock's support and resistance on a real chart, and open a Desk page that screens about 100 stocks and ranks them. Each row shows how much history backs it up, its wall's price range and close, the nearest wall on the other side of price, how many price levels built that wall, its round-number status, and its timeframe breakdown — all fitting on one normal-sized screen with no sideways scrolling needed. Users can also hover a row for the wall-grade detail, repair the Desk's coverage badges, browse past scans, jump from a saved scan into the matching Structure chart, and read Desk data through a connected Claude conversation.
