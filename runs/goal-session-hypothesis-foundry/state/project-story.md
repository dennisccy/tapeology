# Project story so far

Tapeology's newest research chapter, the Hypothesis Foundry, aims to turn every already-approved trading idea sitting in the research notes into one fair, no-cheating test — automatically, without a person picking winners along the way.

## How it has grown

The chapter opened by taking stock: the previous chapter was safely archived, its self-extending loop switched off, and a dated handover recorded, though a setup snag briefly blocked proof screenshots. The second round fixed that snag, gave the Desk page a "Hypothesis Foundry" section naming the active chapter, and built the rulebook for turning an approved idea into a fair, testable specification. The third round showed the chapter's real starting numbers on screen for the first time and built the reading, list-locking, and record-keeping machinery behind the scenes. The fourth round put that machinery on screen in four clearly-labeled practice panels, proving the idea-compiling, idea-reading, list-locking, and full-run-checking pieces all give correct answers, including correctly reversing an answer when a bet direction flips.

The fifth round delivered the chapter's centerpiece: the one real, permanently-recorded decision. All 11 real research ideas on file were checked, and — honestly — none was ready to become a real test yet; each was held back for a specific, disclosed reason. A new "Epoch / Manifest" panel showed this real result on the Desk page, marked with a distinct green banner so it can never be confused with the practice panels.

The sixth round took the era's second and final irreversible step: it actually ran the one real evaluation pass over that frozen, empty research plan. A new "Runner / Checkpoint" panel on the Desk page now shows, with a real timestamp and a real data fingerprint independently checked against the 26-gigabyte research store, that this run happened, found nothing to evaluate (an honest and accepted outcome), and touched zero pieces of protected data while doing it. Checking this closely also caught a real problem: the same summary count is worked out two different ways in two different files, and one of those files can no longer be edited because the era just permanently locked it — so the chapter can't yet be called finished. One earlier open question also remains: an early, discarded first attempt at the real result still needs the project owner's sign-off before the chapter can close.

## What it can do today

The product lets an operator open the Desk page and see, in the Hypothesis Foundry section, the whole story so far: the chapter has opened with the old one safely closed, four practice demonstrations proving the underlying machinery works, the one real result (11 ideas checked, none ready yet — an accepted, honest outcome), and now proof that the one real evaluation run over that result has actually happened and touched nothing off-limits. The rest of Tapeology (Desk, Cockpit, Structure Map) is untouched and works as before. Still to come: the final summary screen that shows the whole chapter's honest conclusion.
