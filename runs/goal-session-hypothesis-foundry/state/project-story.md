# Project story so far

Tapeology's newest research chapter, the Hypothesis Foundry, aims to turn every already-approved trading idea sitting in the research notes into one fair, no-cheating test — automatically, without a person picking winners along the way.

## How it has grown

The chapter opened by closing out the previous one and switching off its self-extending loop (round 1), added a Hypothesis Foundry section to the Desk page and the rulebook for turning an approved idea into a fair, testable specification (round 2), showed the chapter's real starting numbers on screen for the first time (round 3), and then proved the underlying machinery correct across four labeled practice panels, including correctly reversing an answer when a bet direction flips (round 4).

The fifth round delivered the centerpiece: the one real, permanently-recorded decision. All 11 real research ideas were checked and — honestly — none was ready to become a real test yet; each was held back for a specific, disclosed reason, shown in a new Epoch/Manifest panel.

The sixth round ran the one real evaluation pass over that frozen, empty research plan, proving with a real timestamp and an independently-checked data fingerprint that the run happened, found nothing to evaluate (an honest, accepted outcome), and touched no protected data. Checking this closely caught a real problem: the same summary count was worked out two different ways in two different files, one of which the chapter had just permanently locked.

The seventh round fixed that problem the only legal way available — moving the calculation into the one place still allowed to change and adding a permanent agreement check. Nothing on screen changed, but the underlying honesty problem closed, with the one un-fixable wrinkle (a frozen duplicate trapped inside the locked file) written down plainly instead of hidden.

The eighth round finished the chapter's last planned piece: a Final Summary screen that ties every earlier panel together in one place. It shows how each of the 11 real research ideas was ruled on, that zero results survived this round, that the record-keeping check is intact, and lets an operator click into any idea to read its full written reasoning — the mechanism it tested, the reviewer's note, and the exact quoted evidence behind it. A careful review of the new screen caught and fixed one honesty gap before it shipped (an overly strong "complete" claim was softened to match the honest zero-result wording already used elsewhere on the page).

With this piece done, everything the chapter set out to build now works and has been checked twice. What is left is not more building — it is two decisions only the project owner can make: whether to accept an early, discarded first attempt at the real result, and whether it is fine that opening the page quietly writes a small technical bookkeeping file. Work is paused until those two calls are made.

## What it can do today

The product lets an operator open the Desk page and see the whole Hypothesis Foundry research chapter in one place: the chapter started fresh with the old auto-continuing process turned off, the fair-test rules keep each idea's original timing and direction, the one real result (11 ideas checked, none ready yet — an accepted, honest outcome) is on screen, the one real evaluation pass over that result is confirmed complete, and a Final Summary screen now ties it all together with zero survivors this round plus the full written reasoning behind every idea one click away. The rest of Tapeology (Desk, Cockpit, Structure Map) is untouched and works as before.
