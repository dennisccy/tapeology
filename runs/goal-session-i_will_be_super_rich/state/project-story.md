# Project story so far

Tapeology is a real-time tape-reading tool for US stocks: you point it at one ticker and it tells you, in plain language, what the order flow is doing right now — and how sure it is.

## How it has grown

An earlier run built the **practice mode** first, proving the hard ideas on a deterministic simulator before touching real market data. The single-ticker cockpit came to life — a live quote, a running trade list, the core flow readouts, a confidence score, plain-language observations, and an event log. The product's defining habit of honesty was established early: it recognizes when heavy buying or selling is being *absorbed* (the price refuses to move) rather than mistaking that pressure for control.

This session then brought in real US market data through that same engine without ever inventing a number. A data-source selector (Live, Historical, or Simulated) arrived first, then real historical replays — enter a stock, pick a past date and window, and watch that session's real trades and quotes flow through the unchanged engine. A two-stage aggressor classifier resolved nearly all "unknown" trade sides. A candlestick price chart appeared above the cockpit with colored tape-state markers and a bar-size selector. Pause and Resume let a user freeze the cockpit at any moment. A local-time historical picker with one-click US-session presets fixed an earlier timezone gap.

With twenty journeys passing, a spec expansion raised the bar. The Watch button now always gives feedback within one second. Slow or unreachable backends surface an explicit error. Connection failures are shown rather than swallowed. Invalid input is caught inline. A connected stream with no data shows an honest "waiting" state; a background feed failure surfaces as a clear error, never a frozen screen. A third expansion added vendor responsiveness — trades and quotes fetched concurrently, fetched windows cached for near-instant re-watches, the symbol list pre-loaded at startup, and every vendor call bounded by a real network-level deadline with actionable error messages.

The most recent iteration completed a **time-display overhaul**. The price chart's time axis now shows real market clock times — the simulated chart runs a synthetic session clock anchored to a real market open, while a historical chart shows the exact wall-clock times those trades occurred — formatted as day-month-year hours-minutes-seconds throughout. A single shared date formatter was threaded through every date surface in the product (market status, watched-source label, chart markers and crosshair), and the native calendar picker was replaced by a plain typed day-month-year date field with strict inline validation. Three refinement journeys remain: live replay-speed changes, classifier calibration for real data, and chunked loading of long historical windows.

## What it can do today

The product lets users watch one US stock at a time — in simulated mode, a real past session replayed at any speed, or a real live feed — and read the tape in plain language: buyer control, seller control, bid or ask absorption, or an unclear tape, each with a confidence score, live quote, running trades list, and plain-language observations. Users can search for a stock by name (fast, even immediately after restart), choose the data source, and pick historical windows in their own local time using one-click US-session presets entered via a custom day-month-year date field. A candlestick price chart shows true clock times on its axis in simulated and historical modes with colored tape-state markers and a bar-size selector. Users can pause and resume a running watch. Every Watch click gives immediate feedback. A connected stream with no data shows an honest waiting state; a background failure surfaces as an explicit error. Re-watching the same historical window is near-instant from a local cache. An oversized window returns a clear, actionable error message. Dates appear consistently in day-month-year format across the entire product. 32 of 35 must-have capabilities are complete.
