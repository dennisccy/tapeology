# Project story so far

Tapeology is a real-time tape-reading tool for US stocks: you point it at one ticker and it tells you, in plain language, what the order flow is doing right now — and how sure it is.

## How it has grown

An earlier run built the **practice mode** first, proving the hard ideas on a deterministic simulator before touching real market data. The single-ticker cockpit came to life — a live quote, a running trade list, the core flow readouts, a confidence score, plain-language observations, and an event log. The product's defining habit of honesty was established early: it recognizes when heavy buying or selling is being *absorbed* (the price refuses to move) rather than mistaking that pressure for control.

This session then set out to bring in real US market data through that same engine without ever inventing a number. Real data arrived in stages. First came a **data-source selector** (Live, Historical, or Simulated), each revealing only the controls it needs. Then **real historical replays**: enter a US stock, pick a past date and window, and watch that session's real trades and quotes flow through the unchanged engine — including honest error messages for every dead end (unknown symbol, empty window, closed market). After wiring symbol search and market-clock awareness, the session **completed the live promise**: the cockpit can stream a real ticker live, showing a green "live" light during market hours and an amber "stale" light during any quiet gap — inventing no trades during the lull, and recovering cleanly when real data resumes. Those sixteen journeys reached a milestone at iteration 4.

Iteration 5 tackled the **directional accuracy of real-data prints**: roughly one in five real-market trades had been showing "unknown" — those landing between the bid and ask, or arriving before any quote was in effect. A two-stage aggressor classifier now closes that gap, applying the published quote first and falling back to the tick test when that cannot decide. On the committed real Ford data window, unknown prints dropped from 20% to zero.

Iteration 6 added the **prediction chart**: a candlestick price chart now appears above the cockpit whenever you watch a simulated or replayed stock. The engine accumulates every watched trade price into candle bars at 10, 30, or 60 seconds each, and drops a colored marker at the exact moment the tape state shifts to something meaningful — green for buyer control, red for seller control, amber for absorption. A bar-size selector lets you switch granularity on the fly. The backend data pipeline is fully proven correct (the evaluator probed all five simulation scenarios live and confirmed candles, marker colors, and error handling), the suite grew from 141 to 159 tests, and the frontend builds cleanly. The one remaining gap is a browser screenshot of the actual rendered canvas — a corrupted dev-server cache prevented the browser QA agent from running this round. Capturing those screenshots is the top priority for the next iteration.

## What it can do today

The product lets users watch one US stock at a time — in practice mode, a real past session replayed at a chosen speed, or a real live feed — and read the tape in plain language: buyer control, seller control, bid or ask absorption, or an unclear tape, each with a confidence score, live quote, running trades list, and observations. Users can search for a stock, choose the data source, replay history at any speed, and follow a live market with an honest live/stale signal. The recent-trades list labels most real-market prints as buy or sell. For Simulated and Historical watches, a candlestick price chart appears above the cockpit with colored tape-state markers and a 10/30/60-second bar-size selector — letting users see at a glance whether a marked tape state preceded the next price move.
