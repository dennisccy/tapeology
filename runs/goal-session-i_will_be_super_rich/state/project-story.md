# Project story so far

Tapeology is a real-time tape-reading tool for US stocks: you point it at one ticker and it tells you, in plain language, what the order flow is doing right now — and how sure it is.

## How it has grown

An earlier run built the **practice mode** first, proving the hard ideas on a deterministic simulator before touching real market data. The single-ticker cockpit came to life — a live quote, a running trade list, the core flow readouts, a confidence score, plain-language observations, and an event log. The product's defining habit of honesty was established early: it recognizes when heavy buying or selling is being *absorbed* (the price refuses to move) rather than mistaking that pressure for control.

This session then set out to bring in real US market data through that same engine without ever inventing a number. Real data arrived in stages. First came a **data-source selector** (Live, Historical, or Simulated), each revealing only the controls it needs. Then **real historical replays**: enter a US stock, pick a past date and window, and watch that session's real trades and quotes flow through the unchanged engine — with honest messages for every dead end. The session then **completed the live promise**: a real ticker streams live with a green "live" light, an amber "stale" light during quiet gaps, and zero invented trades during lulls. Those sixteen journeys reached an early milestone.

Later iterations sharpened the accuracy. A two-stage aggressor classifier now resolves nearly all "unknown" trade sides — applying the quote-rule first, then the tick test as a fallback. On the committed real Ford data, unknown prints dropped from 20% to zero. A **candlestick price chart** then appeared above the cockpit, showing bar-by-bar price action with colored tape-state markers for every state shift (emerald buyer control, rose seller control, amber absorption) and a 10/30/60-second bar-size selector. A corrupted dev-server cache initially prevented the chart canvas from being confirmed in the browser; that was resolved in the next iteration, which also shipped **Pause/Resume**: a single button freezes the cockpit and chart at a chosen moment without closing the session, then resumes exactly where things left off.

The final iteration closed the last two gaps. **Local-time historical picking** arrived: instead of hand-converting local time to UTC, you now enter your date and time in your own timezone — a label tells you which zone is in effect — and three one-click US-session presets (market open, close, or full trading day) fill the window for you, each showing both the New York time and your local equivalent. A long-standing bug that silently shifted the fetched window by the local UTC offset was fixed at the source. At the same time, the **real-historical candlestick chart** was render-verified for the first time with genuine browser screenshots of real Ford stock prices drawn as candles with tape-state markers — closing the last evidence gap. All twenty must-have journeys now have positive rendered or automated evidence of passing.

## What it can do today

The product lets users watch one US stock at a time — in practice (simulated) mode, a real past session replayed at any speed, or a real live feed — and read the tape in plain language: buyer control, seller control, bid or ask absorption, or an unclear tape, each with a confidence score, live quote, running trades list, and observations. Users can search for a stock by name, choose the data source, and pick historical windows in their own local time using one-click US-session presets (market open, close, or full trading day) with local-time annotations. In simulated and historical modes, a candlestick price chart appears above the cockpit with colored tape-state markers and a bar-size selector. Users can pause a running watch to study the chart at a specific moment, then resume without losing state or seeing invented data. The live mode shows an honest market-status indicator and handles stale feeds without fabricating trades.
