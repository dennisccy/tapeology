# Project story so far

Tapeology is a market-research tool that studies real price and order-flow data honestly, without ever placing a real trade or giving trading advice.

## How it has grown

Earlier chapters built the Cockpit, Structure, Desk and Referee screens, then added a faster idea-testing pipeline — readiness checks, a tick-pressure reader, a signal matcher, a permanent idea ledger, a walk-forward checker, a sealing Vault, and a Graduation check — opened to a Claude conversation through read-only channels. Later rounds hardened the Graduation check's safety tests, closed the project's oldest timing question, and proved repeat research runs always reproduce exactly. Three pre-declared pilot studies came back honest "not enough data yet" answers, the owner recorded 80 real market days and sealed 21 of them, and a round closed a way someone could have identified a secret recording from its sealing time — completing all ten of the project's original planned capabilities.

A later round tried to speed up the Desk's slow readiness panel; an independent check caught and fixed a real bug in that speed-up before it reached anyone. The round after that built nothing on purpose, because the automatic scheduler sent it out at its lightest, developer-free setting, leaving two known jobs undone.

The next round finally delivered those two jobs: the Desk page's Referee section gained a warning label next to an old, misleading count, and two research-checking tests that had grown so slow they risked crashing the site now finish in seconds instead of tens of minutes. That round's own quality control caught and fixed two of its own mistakes along the way, but it ran out of time before re-checking the tenth capability, "Graduation," so that one item was left with a stale checkmark from weeks earlier and the round stopped to ask the project owner for direction.

This latest round finished that leftover job. It actually re-ran the Graduation check's own test three separate times — three different checkers, all in about a second and a half — and confirmed it still passes, formally replacing the stale checkmark with a fresh one. It also double-checked, from scratch rather than by trusting a report, that two small fixes the project owner made directly (the slow-test speed-up, and a separate bug in the project's own closing checklist) really did not touch anything a user can see, and it re-ran the entire research test suite — nearly 3,500 checks — end to end with zero failures. All ten capabilities are now confirmed working with a fresh checkmark, and nothing was found broken. What is left is not a building task: the project owner needs to make two safety-related decisions — one about how a deleted record could hide that data is sealed, and one about a safety limit inside a research check that nothing currently uses — before this chapter of the project can be called complete.

## What it can do today

The product lets people see, on the Desk page, how much market data is on hand and which research checks remain unmet, with a warning label now clarifying which counts are current. It tracks buying and selling pressure tick by tick, matched to chart signals without looking ahead, and keeps a permanent, unhideable record of every quick trading idea it tests, including three pre-declared pilot studies with their honest answers. A panel shows how ideas hold up over time, and a check reveals whether an idea has "graduated" to a fuller test — that check has now been freshly re-confirmed working. The Vault holds a real batch of recorded market days; sealed ones show only a code name and date, with the sealing time hidden too, and the "a secret stays secret" rule is provably enforced end-to-end. A Claude conversation can read all of this the same way a person would on screen.
