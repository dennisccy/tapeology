# Project story so far

Tapeology is a market-research tool that studies real price and order-flow data honestly, without ever placing a real trade or giving trading advice.

## How it has grown

Earlier chapters built the Cockpit, Structure, Desk and Referee screens, then added a faster idea-testing pipeline — readiness checks, a tick-pressure reader, a signal matcher, a permanent idea ledger, a walk-forward checker, a sealing Vault, and a graduation check — all reachable through a read-only Claude conversation. Later rounds hardened safety tests, closed old timing and privacy questions, ran three honest pilot studies, and had the owner record and seal a real batch of 80 market days — completing all ten of the project's originally planned capabilities.

A speed-up for the Desk page's readiness panel had a bug caught by an independent check before it shipped. The next round built nothing on purpose (an automatic scheduler sent it out at its lightest, work-free setting). The round after finally delivered a warning label on an old, misleading count and sped up two safety tests from tens of minutes to seconds — but ran out of time re-checking the graduation capability and stopped to ask the owner how to handle several small, longstanding open questions about the project's own quality-checking process. The round after that re-ran the graduation check three separate ways and confirmed it still works, but two safety-related questions still needed the owner's ruling, so that round also stopped and waited.

**The owner made those rulings directly**, and the next round re-checked everything from scratch against that decision rather than trusting it on faith: zero blocking open questions, neither of the two safety conditions attached to the ruling had come true, the whole safety test suite passed (nearly 3,500 checks), and all ten capabilities were re-confirmed with fresh evidence, including the graduation check whose checkmark went from weeks-old to fresh. The era was declared finished, pending the owner's final sign-off.

Before that sign-off landed, one more small addition was proposed and approved: a new **Graduation** panel on the Desk page, so the "which ideas have graduated, and which failed for good" answer — until now only checked behind the scenes — becomes something a person, or Claude, can actually see. This latest round built that panel and a matching Claude-readable tool, and checked every number on screen against the underlying record line by line. Along the way it also gave the graduation capability its very first on-screen proof, closing a gap that had stood open since the project's first chapter. Two of the three pictures the new panel's own rules ask for — one showing an empty panel, one showing an idea that failed for good — were not taken yet, so this addition is marked as a work in progress rather than finished. Nothing that already worked broke, and no safety rule was bent.

## What it can do today

The product lets people see, on the Desk page, how much market data is on hand and which research checks remain unmet, with a warning label clarifying which counts are current. It tracks buying and selling pressure tick by tick against chart signals without looking ahead, keeps a permanent record of every quick trading idea it tests (including three pre-declared pilot studies with honest answers), and shows how ideas hold up over time. The Vault holds a real, sealed batch of recorded market days — sealed ones show only a code name and date, never a sealing time. A new Graduation panel is being added to show, in plain view, which ideas have reached which stage and any that failed for good — its full picture-proof is not finished yet. A Claude conversation can read all of this the same way a person would.
