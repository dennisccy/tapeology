# Project story so far

Tapeology is a market-research tool that studies real price and order-flow data honestly, without ever placing a real trade or giving trading advice.

## How it has grown

Earlier chapters built the Cockpit, Structure, Desk and Referee screens, then added a faster idea-testing pipeline — readiness checks, a tick-pressure reader, a signal matcher, a permanent idea ledger, a walk-forward checker, a sealing Vault and a Graduation check — opened to a Claude conversation through read-only channels. Later rounds hardened the Graduation check's safety tests, closed the project's oldest timing question, and proved repeat research runs always reproduce. Three pre-declared pilot studies came back honest "not enough data yet" answers, and the owner recorded 80 real market days into the Vault and sealed 21 of them.

A round closed a way someone could combine the Vault's sealing-time stamps with published counts to identify a secret recording, hiding the stamp down to a plain date. The next round took the owed photograph, added a second practice sealed recording, and proved the "a secret stays secret" rule live — closing the project's last open journey, with all ten planned capabilities confirmed working.

The round after that tried to speed up the Desk's slow readiness panel and tidy some internal bookkeeping. An independent check caught the delivered speed-up carrying a real bug — it would have quietly remembered "zero" forever the first time it ran before real data was ready — and fixed it the same round, before it reached anyone outside the team. Two of that round's own proof screenshots got missed when the checking computer overloaded and briefly went offline mid-round.

This latest round built nothing on purpose: the automatic scheduler sent it out at its lightest setting, with no developer and no reviewer, so the two planned jobs — making the slow research checks finish reliably, and adding a warning label to an old, now-misleading number on the Referee screen — were not attempted. All ten planned capabilities were re-checked and still work: two live in a real browser, the rest through stored replay checks. The round's own quality control caught two problems of its own making: a demo write-up that described an on-screen warning message that isn't actually there yet, and a test report that claimed a screenshot proved something it didn't. Both were caught and written up honestly rather than hidden, and the two missed screenshots from the previous round are still owed. The project owner is now being asked to rule on whether that kind of dev-team honesty gap should count against calling this chapter finished, or whether it just needs the two remaining screenshots and the warning label to wrap up.

## What it can do today

The product lets people see, on the Desk page, how much market data is on hand and which research checks remain unmet. It tracks buying and selling pressure tick by tick, matched to chart signals without looking ahead, and keeps a permanent, unhideable record of every quick trading idea it tests, including three pre-declared pilot studies with their honest answers. A panel shows how ideas hold up over time, and a check reveals whether an idea has "graduated" to a fuller test. The Vault holds a real batch of recorded market days; sealed ones show only a code name and date, with the sealing time hidden too, and the "a secret stays secret" rule is provably enforced end-to-end. A Claude conversation can read all of this the same way a person would on screen.
