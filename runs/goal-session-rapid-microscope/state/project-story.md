# Project story so far

Tapeology is a market-research tool that studies real price and order-flow data honestly, without ever placing a real trade or giving trading advice.

## How it has grown

Earlier chapters built the Cockpit, Structure, Desk and Referee screens, then added a faster idea-testing pipeline — readiness checks, a tick-pressure reader, a signal matcher, a permanent idea ledger, a walk-forward checker, a sealing Vault and a Graduation check — opened to a Claude conversation through read-only channels.

Later rounds hardened the Graduation check's safety tests, closed the project's oldest timing question, and proved repeat research runs always reproduce. Three pre-declared pilot studies came back honest "not enough data yet" answers. The owner then recorded 80 real market days into the Vault and sealed 21 of them, and a round independently re-checked that work — bringing all ten planned capabilities to a working state at once.

The next round closed a way someone could combine the Vault's sealing-time stamps with published counts to identify a secret recording, hiding the stamp to just a date — but the fix briefly broke the very screen it touched before being caught and repaired the same round, owing only a fresh photograph. This latest round took that photograph: the Vault's "Sealed at" column now reads a plain date with no clock time, and a second practice sealed recording lets the "a secret stays secret" rule finally be tested end-to-end, proven live by watching it flip from hidden to revealed only on a real release. That closes the project's last open journey — all ten planned capabilities are now confirmed working. What remains is cleanup: a few older minor safety notes are still open, one of which now needs the owner's decision since real sealed recordings exist, and the Desk's readiness panel is still slow to load.

## What it can do today

The product lets people see, on the Desk page, how much market data is on hand and which research checks remain unmet. It tracks buying and selling pressure tick by tick, matched to chart signals without looking ahead, and keeps a permanent, unhideable record of every quick trading idea it tests, including all three pre-declared pilot studies with their honest answers. A panel shows how ideas hold up over time, and a check reveals whether an idea has "graduated" to a fuller test. The Vault holds a real batch of recorded market days; sealed ones show only a code name and date, with the exact sealing time now hidden too. A Claude conversation can read all of this the same way a person would on screen.
