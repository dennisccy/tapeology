# Project story so far

Tapeology is a market-research tool that studies real price and order-flow data honestly, without ever placing a real trade or giving trading advice.

## How it has grown

Earlier chapters built the Cockpit, Structure, Desk and Referee screens, then added a faster idea-testing pipeline — readiness checks, a tick-pressure reader, a signal matcher, a permanent idea ledger, a walk-forward checker, a sealing Vault and a Graduation check — opened to a Claude conversation through read-only channels.

Later rounds hardened the Graduation check's safety tests, closed the project's oldest timing question, and proved repeat research runs always reproduce. Three pre-declared pilot studies came back honest "not enough data yet" answers. The owner then recorded 80 real market days into the Vault and sealed 21 of them, and an independent check confirmed that work — bringing all ten planned capabilities to a working state at once.

A recent round closed a way someone could combine the Vault's sealing-time stamps with published counts to identify a secret recording, hiding the stamp down to a plain date — the fix briefly broke the very screen it touched before being caught and repaired the same round, owing only a fresh photograph. The next round took that photograph and added a second practice sealed recording so the "a secret stays secret" rule could finally be tested end-to-end, proven live by watching it flip from hidden to revealed only on a real release — closing the project's last open journey, with all ten planned capabilities confirmed working.

This latest round set out to fix the Desk's slow readiness panel and tidy one piece of internal bookkeeping. The panel now remembers its band-touch count after the first time it is worked out, so a repeat look loads fast instead of re-scanning raw tick data every time — and a duplicate internal list that decided which idea needed which kind of supporting data was removed, so the two copies can no longer disagree. Along the way, an independent check caught the delivered speed-up carrying a real bug: it would have quietly remembered "zero" forever the first time it ran before real data was ready, and it was fixed and proven fixed the same round before reaching anyone outside the team. Two of this round's own screenshots — the Desk readiness numbers and the Scout Ledger's idea list — got missed when the checking computer overloaded and briefly went offline mid-round, so a retake is owed next round, alongside making the automatic checks themselves faster so that outage does not happen again.

## What it can do today

The product lets people see, on the Desk page, how much market data is on hand and which research checks remain unmet — now loading its busiest figure faster on a repeat view. It tracks buying and selling pressure tick by tick, matched to chart signals without looking ahead, and keeps a permanent, unhideable record of every quick trading idea it tests, including all three pre-declared pilot studies with their honest answers. A panel shows how ideas hold up over time, and a check reveals whether an idea has "graduated" to a fuller test. The Vault holds a real batch of recorded market days; sealed ones show only a code name and date, with the exact sealing time hidden too, and the "a secret stays secret" rule is now provably enforced end-to-end. A Claude conversation can read all of this the same way a person would on screen.
