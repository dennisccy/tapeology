# Project story so far

Tapeology is a market-research tool that studies real price and order-flow data honestly, without ever placing a real trade or giving trading advice.

## How it has grown

Earlier chapters built the Cockpit, Structure, Desk and Referee screens, then added a faster idea-testing pipeline — readiness checks, a tick-pressure reader, a signal matcher, a permanent idea ledger, a walk-forward checker, a sealing Vault and a Graduation check — opened to a Claude conversation through read-only channels. Later rounds hardened the Graduation check's safety tests, closed the project's oldest timing question, and proved repeat research runs always reproduce. Three pre-declared pilot studies came back honest "not enough data yet" answers, and the owner recorded 80 real market days into the Vault and sealed 21 of them. A round closed a way someone could combine the Vault's sealing-time stamps with published counts to identify a secret recording, and the next round proved the "a secret stays secret" rule live — closing the project's last open journey, with all ten planned capabilities confirmed working.

A later round tried to speed up the Desk's slow readiness panel; an independent check caught a real bug in the delivered speed-up (it would have quietly remembered "zero" forever the first time it ran before real data was ready) and fixed it the same round, before it reached anyone outside the team. The round after that built nothing on purpose — the automatic scheduler sent it out at its lightest setting, with no developer — so two planned jobs (making the slow research checks finish reliably, and adding a warning label to an old, misleading number on the Referee screen) went untouched, though all ten capabilities were re-confirmed still working.

This latest round finally delivered both of those jobs. The Desk page's Referee Registry section now carries a short warning sentence next to its old dataset/trade counts, telling readers that count is out of date compared to the newer, more complete system. And two of the project's own automated research-checking test files, which had grown so slow (14 and 28 minutes each) that they risked crashing the live site during testing, now finish in about nine seconds combined. All ten planned capabilities are still confirmed working, with zero regressions. The round's own quality control caught and fixed two of its own mistakes along the way — a safety test that could never actually fail, and a screenshot wrongly cited as proof of something it didn't show. One capability ("Graduation") didn't get re-checked this round because time ran out. The project owner is now being asked to make a few decisions — mainly whether some ongoing dev-team process gaps should count against calling this chapter "finished" — before the last two small clean-up jobs can be built.

## What it can do today

The product lets people see, on the Desk page, how much market data is on hand and which research checks remain unmet — now with an added warning label clarifying which counts are current. It tracks buying and selling pressure tick by tick, matched to chart signals without looking ahead, and keeps a permanent, unhideable record of every quick trading idea it tests, including three pre-declared pilot studies with their honest answers. A panel shows how ideas hold up over time, and a check reveals whether an idea has "graduated" to a fuller test. The Vault holds a real batch of recorded market days; sealed ones show only a code name and date, with the sealing time hidden too, and the "a secret stays secret" rule is provably enforced end-to-end. A Claude conversation can read all of this the same way a person would on screen.
