# Project story so far

Tapeology is a research desk for reading a stock's tape and structure — never a trading system, and never a source of advice.

## How it has grown

Before this chapter began, the desk already let someone watch a simulated ticker's buy-and-sell pressure live, load a real stock's chart with support-and-resistance walls, and run a daily screen across roughly a hundred large companies with a ranked briefing and forward-looking return numbers. This chapter, "The Playbook," teaches the desk to recognize classic intraday chart patterns from a well-known trading book and honestly report what happened afterward — no predictions, no advice.

The opening rounds built the plumbing: a checkup confirmed everything already shipped still worked, the desk learned to spot and record its first pattern (an opening-range breakout, honest when there wasn't enough history), it learned to measure what happened afterward using the desk's own trusted rules, and the Desk page gained a "Playbook Signals" section — pick a date, press Run Playbook, read the results.

The next two rounds taught four more patterns — Jump-Base Explosion, its mirror Drop-Base Implosion, Cup and Handle, and Capitulation (a panic decline that reverses, with a "recent climax" tag for signals that follow one). Careful checks caught and fixed two real problems before anyone saw them — a mislabeled pattern, and summary text lagging behind the growing list.

This round finished the full set: Range Trade (a bounce off a well-tested support or resistance level) and Double Top / Double Bottom (a two-peak or two-valley reversal). All nine of the book's patterns now sit together on the Desk page. A careful mid-round check caught a real bug — an early Range Trade reading was too loose and could, in a rare case, show a stop level on the wrong side of the trade — fixed before shipping, with practice screenshots retaken to prove it. Three small judgment calls from this round are written down for the project owner to weigh in on when convenient; none change anything already on screen.

## What it can do today

The product lets users watch a simulated ticker and see whether buyers or sellers are in control on a live price chart, load a real company's stock chart with support and resistance zones drawn on it, and run the desk's daily screen for a ranked briefing with forward-looking return numbers and past runs. It also lets users open the Desk page, pick a date, and see every one of the book's nine chart patterns found for that day — opening-range breakouts, Jump-Base Explosions, Drop-Base Implosions, Cup and Handles, Capitulation reversals, Range Trades, and Double Top/Double Bottom reversals — each measured against a random-chance baseline.
