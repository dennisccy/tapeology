# Project story so far

Tapeology is a tape-reading tool that watches stock trades as they happen and tells you, in plain language, what the market is actually doing right now — whether buyers are in control, sellers are pressing, or the tape is unclear — and lets you declare, track, and now close your own trading thesis against that live read.

## How it has grown

The product started as a real-time tape cockpit. Type in a ticker, hit Watch, and the screen fills with live data: recent trades colour-coded by side, 14 calculated tape features, a tape-state verdict with a confidence score, and a price chart with true clock-time candles. The engine is built to be honest: high aggression with no price move reads as absorption rather than control; a weak or mixed tape reads as unclear. Symbol search, historical replay, live streaming, and pause/resume all work without tearing down the cockpit.

Early iterations built the thesis layer — a "Declare a thesis" strip on the cockpit where you choose a setup type, direction, and required invalidation price. The verdict engine evaluates your thesis continuously: an absorption-reversal only confirms when price actually flips; a level-break stays pending until price crosses the declared level; a trend-continuation rejects while the opposite side has control; a failed-move fade confirms while the failed push is being absorbed. A bad input is refused immediately with a plain-language message.

After resolving several tooling issues (a missing database migration, a build-tool conflict that corrupted the frontend's output, and a stale QA server that produced misleading captures), the product reached a reliable verification baseline. By iteration 5, the verdict strip rendered live in a real browser for the first time with moment-correct pixel evidence across all five verdict states: pending, confirming, weakening (amber), rejecting, and the terminal invalidated badge.

Iteration 6 delivered the remaining four verdict-state captures. Two correctness defects were fixed: a "making progress in your direction" statement that read as met on an adverse tape, and a failed-move-fade setup that was watching the wrong absorption side. With those fixes on disk, four verdict journeys flipped to passing, including the amber weakening chip rendering for the first time. One re-capture (the failed-move fade confirming during absorption, and the adverse-tape statement) was deferred because the QA server had not been restarted before the captures; the fixes were confirmed on disk and unit-proven.

Iteration 7 completed that re-capture and added user-facing thesis resolution. The QA server was restarted and its identity verified before any capture, confirming the iter-6 fixes were live. The failed-move fade now correctly shows as confirming during the bid-absorption phase (not waiting for the price reclaim), and a trend-continuation thesis correctly shows its "making progress" statement as violated when the tape is actively working against you. On top of the re-captures, two new buttons — "Played out" and "Abandon" — now appear on the active thesis strip. Clicking either one saves the resolution with logical and wall timestamps, shows a plain-language confirmation, and returns the cockpit to the declare affordance so the next thesis can begin. System-owned outcomes (invalidated, expired) remain untouchable by the user. This closes the full thesis lifecycle in the UI. The same fresh captures also revealed a new honesty defect: the "making progress" statement reads violated even on a clean confirming tape where buyers clearly dominate, because the evaluation code checks for any adverse-side activity before weighing the dominant side — this will be fixed next.

## What it can do today

The product lets users watch any stock ticker (simulated, historical, or live) and see a real-time cockpit that identifies buyer control, seller control, bid absorption, ask absorption, and unclear tape with confidence scores. Users can replay historical sessions, stream live tickers, search for symbols, pause and resume a watch without losing state, and view a price chart with tape-state markers on true clock-time candles. Users can declare a trading thesis — choosing a setup type, direction, and invalidation price — and watch the tape judged live with a colour-coded verdict badge and plain-language evidence across all five verdict states. Users can now close their own thesis by marking it "played out" or "abandoning" it, with timestamps recorded and the strip freed for the next declaration. System-owned outcomes (invalidated, expired) are protected from user override.
