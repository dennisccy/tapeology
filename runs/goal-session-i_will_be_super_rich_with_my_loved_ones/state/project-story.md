# Project story so far

Tapeology is a tape-reading and trade-journaling tool that watches stock trades as they happen, tells you what the market is actually doing right now, keeps an honest restart-proof record of every thesis you have ever declared against that read, and now lets you replay your setup grammar over a historical window to see how it would have played out.

## How it has grown

The product started as a real-time tape cockpit. Type in a ticker, hit Watch, and the screen fills with live data: recent trades colour-coded by side, 14 calculated tape features, a tape-state verdict with a confidence score, and a price chart with true clock-time candles. The engine is built to be honest: high aggression with no price move reads as absorption rather than control; a weak or mixed tape reads as unclear. Symbol search, historical replay, live streaming, and pause/resume all work without tearing down the cockpit.

Early iterations built the thesis layer — a "Declare a thesis" strip on the cockpit where you choose a setup type, direction, and required invalidation price. The verdict engine reached a reliable baseline: all five verdict states render in real browser pixels. Iterations 6 through 11 completed thesis resolution, all verdict scenarios, entry and exit mark recording with spread and realized R, thesis survival across watch interruptions, geometry drawn on the price chart, and honest entry-risk advisory chips at declaration.

Iteration 12 turned Tapeology into a two-page product with a persistent navigation bar and a filterable Journal. Iteration 13 completed the journal detail page. Iteration 14 finished the review pillar: statement status badges, two grade labels (outcome and process, independently scored), and a Save Review button with mistake tags from the backend taxonomy. Iteration 15 opened the evidence layer with per-horizon excursion outcomes in R. Iteration 16 completed the evidence layer by adding an Analytics view to the Journal — honest, segregated statistics partitioned by data feed and config fingerprint, with abandonment always in the denominator and spread cost always shown.

Iteration 17 went under the hood for the first time in the session. The tape-reading engine received a significant internal improvement: rolling-feature maintenance is now truly incremental across window evictions instead of quietly falling back to a full re-scan on every event once old data aged out. The result is an approximately 18-fold speedup on dense real-data streams. A real ten-minute recording of Procter & Gamble trading from the consolidated SIP feed is now committed to the project so CI can confirm both speed and correctness without a live market account.

Iteration 18 used that committed recording to build the Studies page — the last piece of the evidence layer before the cue layer is allowed to exist. A "Studies" link that had been greyed out in the navigation bar since the beginning is now live. You can pick the committed PG reference window (no credentials needed), a built-in simulated scenario, or any past date and symbol, then click "Run study." The page shows the job moving through Queued, Running, and Done on its own. When it finishes you can read a table of individual occurrences with their R basis and verdict, and a side-by-side comparison of how the setup fared versus a randomly-timed null baseline over the same window — per horizon, with truncated results always counted separately. The same seed always produces the same baseline, so re-running a study is perfectly reproducible. You can cancel a running study and see a clearly-labelled partial result. An automated CI test now pins the exact numbers from the reference window so the result is verified on every build without credentials. The backend is exhaustively proven and a browser walk-through of the page itself is the one remaining step, expected in the next iteration.

## What it can do today

The product lets users watch any stock ticker (simulated, historical, or live) and see a real-time cockpit identifying buyer control, seller control, bid and ask absorption, and unclear tape. Users can replay historical sessions, stream live tickers, search for symbols, pause and resume a watch, and view a price chart with tape-state markers at true clock time. Users can declare a trading thesis, watch it judged live across all five verdict states with plain-language evidence, mark their actual entry and exit verbatim, see the realized move in R units, and close a thesis honestly. Users can navigate to a persistent Journal, open any thesis for a full review with frozen statements, final-status badges, outcome and process grades, execution checks, and saved mistake tags. On any ended thesis, users can read per-horizon excursion outcomes in R anchored separately at confirmation and at entry. Users can switch the Journal to an Analytics view and read honest, segregated statistics of all their recorded theses. Users can now also navigate to the Studies page, run a deterministic replay study of a setup grammar over a chosen window, and compare the results side-by-side with a seeded random-time null baseline — with honesty stamps, n counts, and a "Descriptive only — not trading advice" label always visible.
