# Project story so far

Tapeology is a tape-reading and trade-journaling tool that watches stock trades as they happen, tells you what the market is actually doing right now, keeps an honest restart-proof record of every thesis you have ever declared against that read, and lets you replay your setup grammar over a historical window to measure how it would have played out.

## How it has grown

The product started as a real-time tape cockpit. Type in a ticker, hit Watch, and the screen fills with live data: recent trades colour-coded by side, 14 calculated tape features, a tape-state verdict with a confidence score, and a price chart with true clock-time candles. The engine is built to be honest: high aggression with no price move reads as absorption rather than control; a weak or mixed tape reads as unclear.

Early iterations built the thesis layer and a persistent Journal with filterable rows, review grades, execution checks, saved mistake tags, and per-horizon excursion outcomes in R. Iteration 17 made the tape-reading engine truly incremental — roughly 18x faster on dense real-data streams. Iterations 18 and 19 delivered the Studies page: deterministic replay studies against a seeded random-time null baseline, with honesty stamps and sample caveats always in view.

Iterations 20 through 26 completed the full cue layer. The product gained a management stance (Thesis Intact, Weakening, or Invalidated, with live distance-to-invalidation in dollars and R); an eight-item entry checklist with live measured margins that immediately shows "NO FRESH TAPE" if the feed pauses; setup-forming hints with measured plain-language evidence and honest study citations; a live feed-basis badge; and a product-wide copy-discipline sweep with a sound-toggle that is always visible on the cockpit and off by default.

Iterations 27 and 28 closed the remaining real-data verification legs without touching a line of application code — pulling 24,619 real Apple trades, proving true-clock chart axes, bounded timeout errors, a stream-closed state, and a clearly visible "Couldn't connect" failure panel in two md5-verified stills.

Iteration 29, run during open US market hours on Tuesday 16 June 2026, closed the two legs that could only be captured on a live exchange feed — and in doing so completed every Must-have journey in the product. A real Alpaca IEX socket was used to watch IBM and Ford: the status indicator correctly turned green ("live"), switched to amber ("stale") each time the real feed went quiet for more than ten seconds, and snapped back to green the moment new market data arrived — with the recent-trades count frozen throughout every quiet spell, proving the app fabricated no data at any point. The live cockpit showed an "IEX (live)" badge with a plain disclosure note explaining how live and historical data differ. A thesis declared during the live watch was stamped "iex" in the Journal, keeping live and historical records strictly separate. A credentialed live-socket integration test passed against the real socket. No application code was changed — the desired byte-identical outcome held. With J-15 passing, every Must-have journey (J-01 through J-68) is now proven.

## What it can do today

The product lets users watch any stock ticker (simulated, historical SIP replay, or live IEX) and see a real-time cockpit labeled with its data-feed basis. While live, the status indicator turns amber and reads "stale" whenever the exchange feed goes quiet, then recovers to green the moment new data arrives — no prices or trades are ever invented. Users can replay historical sessions against real SIP data, pick a window in their local timezone, and change replay speed without restarting. Honest panels explain market-closed, unknown-symbol, and empty-window states. Users can declare a trading thesis and watch it judged live across all five verdict states, mark actual entry and exit, see the realized move in R, and close the thesis honestly. An eight-item entry checklist with live measured margins shows an immediate freshness warning when the feed pauses. Management stance and setup-forming hints (with measured evidence and honest study citations) complete the cue layer. Every hint is logged in the Journal's Hints view. Users can browse the Journal for a full trade history with review grades, excursion outcomes, and analytics partitioned by data feed and config, and can run replay studies against a seeded random-time null baseline. The live-declared journal row is stamped with the IEX feed basis, keeping live and historical records strictly separate with no pooling.
