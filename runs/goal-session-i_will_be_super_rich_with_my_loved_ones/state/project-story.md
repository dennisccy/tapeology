# Project story so far

Tapeology is a tape-reading tool that watches stock trades as they happen and tells you, in plain language, what the market is actually doing right now — whether buyers are in control, sellers are pressing, or the tape is unclear — and lets you declare and track your own trading thesis against that live read.

## How it has grown

The product started as a real-time tape cockpit. Type in a ticker, hit Watch, and the screen fills with live data: recent trades colour-coded by side, 14 calculated tape features, a tape-state verdict with a confidence score, and a price chart with true clock-time candles. The engine is built to be honest: high aggression with no price move reads as absorption rather than control; a weak or mixed tape reads as unclear. Symbol search, historical replay, live streaming, and pause/resume all work without tearing down the cockpit.

Iterations 1 and 2 built the thesis layer — a "Declare a thesis" strip that appears while watching a ticker. Choose a setup type, direction, and required invalidation price; the strip expands to show live expected-behaviour statements, a pending verdict badge, and an honesty stamp. Bad inputs are refused immediately with a plain-language message. The verdict engine was built and unit-tested across all four setup types: an absorption-reversal only confirms when price actually flips; a level-break stays pending until price crosses the declared level; a trend-continuation rejects while the opposite side has control; a failed-move fade confirms while the failed push is being absorbed.

Iteration 3 cleared a tooling conflict that blocked browser verification. Iteration 4 assembled the frontend verdict chip (slate for pending, emerald for confirming, amber for weakening, rose for rejecting, rose-with-ring for invalidated) and the journal timeline — but a missing database migration caused every thesis declaration to return a server error, so no verdict chip reached the browser.

Iteration 5 fixed that. The database upgrade runs automatically on startup, atomic declaration was implemented, and the verdict engine ran in a real browser for the first time: declare, watch it go from pending to confirming or rejecting, see the terminal invalidated badge with the offending print recorded. Four journeys moved to passing and five more moved from failing to partial, each within one moment-correct browser capture.

Iteration 6 delivered those captures. Two correctness defects were fixed first: a "making progress in your direction" statement that read as met even when the tape was actively working against a long thesis, and a failed-move-fade setup whose absorption side was inverted — a long fade was watching the wrong side of the tape. With those fixed, four of the five targeted verdict transitions passed browser verification with moment-correct, evaluator-opened pixel evidence. Most notably, the amber WEAKENING chip rendered in the browser for the first time — a trend-continuation thesis confirmed during buyer control, then the tape went neutral, and the verdict transitioned to weakening with a distinct "control faded" evidence line. All five verdict states (pending, confirming, weakening, rejecting, invalidated) have now been seen in real browser captures. One target journey (failed-move fade) and one statement-honesty re-capture remain pending solely because the QA server was not restarted before the captures; the code fixes are on disk and unit-proven.

## What it can do today

The product lets users watch any stock ticker (simulated, historical, or live) and see a real-time cockpit that identifies buyer control, seller control, bid absorption, ask absorption, and unclear tape with confidence scores. Users can replay historical sessions, stream live tickers, search for symbols, pause and resume a watch without losing state, and view a price chart with tape-state markers on true clock-time candles. Users can declare a trade thesis on a watched ticker — choosing a setup type, direction, and invalidation price — and watch the tape judged live with a colour-coded verdict badge and plain-language evidence. Incoherent inputs are refused with a clear inline message. When the tape invalidates a thesis, a terminal rose badge appears with the offending print recorded. All five verdict states have now rendered in real browser captures: pending, confirming, weakening (amber), rejecting, and invalidated.
