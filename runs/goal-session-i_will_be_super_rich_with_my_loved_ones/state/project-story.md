# Project story so far

Tapeology is a tape-reading and trade-journaling tool that watches stock trades as they happen, tells you what the market is actually doing right now, and keeps an honest, restart-proof record of every thesis you have ever declared against that read.

## How it has grown

The product started as a real-time tape cockpit. Type in a ticker, hit Watch, and the screen fills with live data: recent trades colour-coded by side, 14 calculated tape features, a tape-state verdict with a confidence score, and a price chart with true clock-time candles. The engine is built to be honest: high aggression with no price move reads as absorption rather than control; a weak or mixed tape reads as unclear. Symbol search, historical replay, live streaming, and pause/resume all work without tearing down the cockpit.

Early iterations built the thesis layer — a "Declare a thesis" strip on the cockpit where you choose a setup type, direction, and required invalidation price. The verdict engine reached a reliable baseline: all five verdict states — confirming, rejecting, weakening, invalidated, and pending — render in real browser pixels. Iterations 6 and 7 completed thesis resolution and the remaining verdict scenarios. Iteration 8 added entry and exit mark recording with spread-at-mark and realized R. Iteration 9 made an entry-marked thesis survive a watch interruption. Iteration 10 brought thesis geometry onto the price chart. Iteration 11 added honest entry-risk advisory chips at declaration — six conditions evaluated at the moment you declare, each chip carrying its measured margin in plain language.

Iteration 12 turned Tapeology into a two-page product: a persistent navigation bar (Cockpit, Journal, Studies) and a filterable Journal table of every thesis ever declared, with verbatim outcome reasons, confirmed honest across a real backend restart. Iteration 13 completed the journal's detail half — click any journal row to open a full review page showing frozen expected-behaviour statements, the complete verdict timeline at true clock time, entry/exit marks, frozen risk flags, and four machine-derived execution checks computed once at resolution and never recomputed at read.

Iteration 14 completed the review pillar. Each frozen statement in the thesis detail now shows its final status — met, not met, or violated — recorded at the exact moment the thesis closed, never derived again at read time. Two grade labels appear beside it: one for the outcome (did the thesis hold or fail?), one for the process (was the entry disciplined, flagged, or did an execution check fail?). Being invalidated alone never counts as a process failure — only failing your own execution checks does. And for the first time, the Save Review button is live: pick your mistake tags from the backend taxonomy, add a note if you select "Other", and save — the thesis is marked as reviewed and your selection is preserved exactly as you entered it. All four pillars of the declared-thesis lifecycle — declare, judge, resolve, review — are now operational end to end.

## What it can do today

The product lets users watch any stock ticker (simulated, historical, or live) and see a real-time cockpit identifying buyer control, seller control, bid absorption, ask absorption, and unclear tape with confidence scores. Users can replay historical sessions, stream live tickers, search for symbols, pause and resume a watch without losing state, and view a price chart with tape-state markers on true clock-time candles. Users can declare a trading thesis, watch it judged live across all five verdict states with plain-language evidence, see the declared thesis geometry drawn on the chart, mark their actual entry and exit prices verbatim, see the realized move in R units, close a thesis as played out or abandoned, survive a watch interruption with the thesis intact, and receive honest amber entry-risk chips at declaration. Users can navigate to a persistent Journal page listing all declared theses, click any row to open a full detail page showing the frozen statements with their persisted final-status badges, the outcome and process grade with evidence, risk flags, entry/exit marks, machine-derived execution checks with pre-selected mistake-tag suggestions, and a Save Review button to confirm mistake tags and a note that permanently marks the thesis as reviewed.
