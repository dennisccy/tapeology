# Project story so far

Tapeology is a tape-reading tool that watches stock trades as they happen and tells you, in plain language, what the market is actually doing right now — whether buyers are in control, sellers are pressing, or the tape is unclear — and lets you declare, track, mark, and close your own trading thesis against that live read.

## How it has grown

The product started as a real-time tape cockpit. Type in a ticker, hit Watch, and the screen fills with live data: recent trades colour-coded by side, 14 calculated tape features, a tape-state verdict with a confidence score, and a price chart with true clock-time candles. The engine is built to be honest: high aggression with no price move reads as absorption rather than control; a weak or mixed tape reads as unclear. Symbol search, historical replay, live streaming, and pause/resume all work without tearing down the cockpit.

Early iterations built the thesis layer — a "Declare a thesis" strip on the cockpit where you choose a setup type, direction, and required invalidation price. After resolving several tooling issues (a stale build cache that broke browser verification twice, a missing database migration, and a correctness defect in statement evaluation), the verdict engine reached a reliable baseline. By iteration 5 all five verdict states — confirming, rejecting, weakening, invalidated, and pending — had rendered in real browser pixels. Iteration 6 and 7 completed the remaining captures against canary-verified fresh servers and added user-facing thesis resolution: "Played out" and "Abandon" buttons save the outcome with timestamps and free the strip for the next thesis.

Iteration 8 fixed a subtle honesty gap in statement evaluation — the dominance rule now compares favorable-vs-adverse price impact by plain magnitude, so a buyer-dominant confirming tape correctly reads the statement as met rather than violated. The same iteration introduced the journaling half of a trade: Mark entry and Mark exit controls record your actual prices verbatim with spread-at-mark. Once an entry exists, the Abandon button disappears so an open position cannot be silently discarded. After both marks are recorded, the strip shows the realized move in R units labeled as a journaled measurement.

Iteration 9 tackled a critical lifecycle gap: a trader who stops the watch mid-position used to lose their thesis entirely. Now an entry-marked thesis survives the interruption. After you stop watching, the cockpit strip honestly shows "NOT EVALUATED" with a backend message telling you which source to re-watch to resume. When you re-watch the same source, the thesis picks up where it left off — with exactly one "watch restarted" gap event recorded in the timeline so the interrupted period is always visible. An unmarked thesis still auto-expires on stop, but now with an explicit reason that distinguishes a user stop from a stream that ran out naturally. The cross-source mismatch leg is also protected: a watch of a different source is never evaluated against a thesis declared on another.

## What it can do today

The product lets users watch any stock ticker (simulated, historical, or live) and see a real-time cockpit identifying buyer control, seller control, bid absorption, ask absorption, and unclear tape with confidence scores. Users can replay historical sessions, stream live tickers, search for symbols, pause and resume a watch without losing state, and view a price chart with tape-state markers on true clock-time candles. Users can declare a trading thesis — choosing setup type, direction, and invalidation price — and watch the tape judged live across all five verdict states with plain-language evidence. Users can mark their actual entry and exit prices verbatim, see the realized move in R units, and close a thesis as played out or abandoned. If an entry mark exists, the Abandon button is hidden. If the watch is interrupted while an entry mark is present, the thesis survives as "not currently evaluated" and resumes with an explicit recorded gap when the same source is re-watched.
