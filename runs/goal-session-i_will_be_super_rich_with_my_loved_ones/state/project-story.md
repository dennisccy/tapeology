# Project story so far

Tapeology is a tape-reading and trade-journaling tool that watches stock trades as they happen, tells you what the market is actually doing right now, and keeps an honest, restart-proof record of every thesis you have ever declared against that read.

## How it has grown

The product started as a real-time tape cockpit. Type in a ticker, hit Watch, and the screen fills with live data: recent trades colour-coded by side, 14 calculated tape features, a tape-state verdict with a confidence score, and a price chart with true clock-time candles. The engine is built to be honest: high aggression with no price move reads as absorption rather than control; a weak or mixed tape reads as unclear. Symbol search, historical replay, live streaming, and pause/resume all work without tearing down the cockpit.

Early iterations built the thesis layer — a "Declare a thesis" strip on the cockpit where you choose a setup type, direction, and required invalidation price. After resolving a correctness defect in statement evaluation, the verdict engine reached a reliable baseline: all five verdict states — confirming, rejecting, weakening, invalidated, and pending — render in real browser pixels with the underlying persistence layer proven against committed schema fixtures. Iterations 6 and 7 completed the remaining verdict captures, added user-facing thesis resolution ("Played out" and "Abandon" buttons), and finished the failed-move fade and absorption-reversal journeys.

Iteration 8 introduced the journaling half of a trade: Mark entry and Mark exit controls record actual prices verbatim with spread-at-mark, showing the realized move in R units. Once an entry exists, the Abandon button disappears so an open position cannot be silently discarded. Iteration 9 tackled the lifecycle: an entry-marked thesis now survives a watch interruption as "NOT EVALUATED," and re-watching reattaches it with exactly one recorded gap event. Iteration 10 brought the thesis onto the chart itself — labeled invalidation and level price-lines, verdict-transition markers, entry marker, and first-confirmation marker, all computed once on the server and drawn verbatim.

Iteration 11 completed entry-risk assessment. When you declare a thesis, the product now instantly evaluates six risk conditions — chasing an extended move, invalidation too tight for the spread, slow or illiquid tape, not yet warmed up, or going against the tape's expected direction — and shows amber advisory chips for any that fire, each with the measured margin in plain language. A clean declare produces no chips and no false reassurance.

Iteration 12 turned Tapeology from a single-screen cockpit into a two-page product. A persistent top navigation bar now sits above every page: Cockpit · Journal · Studies (Studies is shown but disabled until its page is built — no dead links). Clicking "Journal" opens a new page listing every thesis ever declared — active, resolved, expired, and abandoned — in a filterable table. The declared date, ticker, bound source, data feed, setup, direction, and status are shown verbatim from the persisted store. If the backend restarted while an active thesis was open, that row now shows the exact reason "Thesis expired on restart — the watch that declared it is no longer running," distinct from stream-closed or user-stop reasons. A real backend restart mid-QA-run confirmed the honesty live: the resolved row was byte-identical before and after the restart, and no history was lost or rewritten.

## What it can do today

The product lets users watch any stock ticker (simulated, historical, or live) and see a real-time cockpit identifying buyer control, seller control, bid absorption, ask absorption, and unclear tape with confidence scores. Users can replay historical sessions, stream live tickers, search for symbols, pause and resume a watch without losing state, and view a price chart with tape-state markers on true clock-time candles. Users can declare a trading thesis, watch it judged live across all five verdict states with plain-language evidence, see the declared thesis geometry drawn on the chart, mark their actual entry and exit prices verbatim, see the realized move in R units, close a thesis as played out or abandoned, survive a watch interruption with the thesis intact, and receive honest amber entry-risk chips at declaration. Users can now also navigate to a persistent Journal page and browse all their declared theses — past and present — in a filterable table that survives backend restarts with complete honesty about what happened and why.
