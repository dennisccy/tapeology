# Project story so far

Tapeology is a tape-reading and trade-journaling tool that watches stock trades as they happen, tells you what the market is actually doing right now, keeps an honest restart-proof record of every thesis you have ever declared against that read, and lets you replay your setup grammar over a historical window to measure how it would have played out.

## How it has grown

The product started as a real-time tape cockpit. Type in a ticker, hit Watch, and the screen fills with live data: recent trades colour-coded by side, 14 calculated tape features, a tape-state verdict with a confidence score, and a price chart with true clock-time candles. The engine is built to be honest: high aggression with no price move reads as absorption rather than control; a weak or mixed tape reads as unclear.

Early iterations built the thesis layer — a "Declare a thesis" strip where you choose a setup type, direction, and required invalidation price. All five verdict states render in real browser pixels. The review pillar followed: a persistent Journal with filterable rows, a review-detail page with frozen statements, independently scored outcome and process grades, execution checks, saved mistake tags from the backend taxonomy, and per-horizon excursion outcomes in R. Iteration 17 made the tape-reading engine truly incremental (~18x speedup on dense real-data streams). Iterations 18 and 19 delivered the Studies page: deterministic replay studies against a seeded random-time null baseline, with honesty stamps, sample caveats, and a "Descriptive only — not trading advice" label always in view. That closed the "evidence before cues" gate, allowing the cue layer to begin.

Iterations 20 through 23 completed the full cue layer. The product gained a management stance (Thesis Intact, Weakening, or Invalidated, with live distance-to-invalidation in dollars and R); an eight-item entry checklist with live measured margins that immediately shows "NO FRESH TAPE" if the feed pauses; and setup-forming hints — amber "Setup forming" cards that appear when a recognisable absorption or control pattern sustains past a short dwell period, show measured plain-language evidence, cite the user's own study results verbatim (or say plainly "no studied baseline"), and let users one-click prefill the declare form without ever creating a thesis automatically. Every hint is permanently logged in a Hints tab in the Journal.

Iteration 24 completed the last display gap in the cue and data-honesty layer. The cockpit now shows a feed-basis badge beside the stream-status indicator: "Simulated", "IEX (live)", or "SIP (consolidated)" depending on the watch mode. On a live IEX watch, a one-line disclosure note explains that live verdicts read the IEX single-venue feed while historical replay and studies use SIP. When no ticker is being watched the badge is absent — it never guesses. The hints log gained a "Feed" column so every stored hint row shows which data basis it came from. Under the hood, the two divergent copies of the feed-label mapping were consolidated into one config-aligned function, so switching from IEX to SIP requires a single config change with zero relabeling code.

## What it can do today

The product lets users watch any stock ticker (simulated, historical, or live) and see a real-time cockpit labeling the data-feed basis alongside live tape-state verdicts, confidence, quotes, recent trades, 14 features, and a price chart at true clock time. Users can replay historical sessions, stream live tickers, search for symbols, pause and resume a watch, and choose their data source. Users can declare a trading thesis and watch it judged live across all five verdict states, mark actual entry and exit, see the realized move in R, and close a thesis honestly. Users can see an eight-item entry checklist with live measured margins and an immediate freshness warning when the feed goes stale. Users can see a management stance while holding a journaled position, and receive setup-forming hints with measured evidence and honest study citations. Every hint is logged in the Journal's Hints view with a feed-basis stamp. Users can browse the Journal for a full trade history with review grades, excursion outcomes, and honest analytics partitioned by data feed and config fingerprint. Users can run replay studies against a seeded random-time null baseline and compare results side by side.
