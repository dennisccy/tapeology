# Project story so far

Tapeology is a tape-reading and trade-journaling tool that watches stock trades as they happen, tells you what the market is actually doing right now, and keeps an honest, restart-proof record of every thesis you have ever declared against that read.

## How it has grown

The product started as a real-time tape cockpit. Type in a ticker, hit Watch, and the screen fills with live data: recent trades colour-coded by side, 14 calculated tape features, a tape-state verdict with a confidence score, and a price chart with true clock-time candles. The engine is built to be honest: high aggression with no price move reads as absorption rather than control; a weak or mixed tape reads as unclear. Symbol search, historical replay, live streaming, and pause/resume all work without tearing down the cockpit.

Early iterations built the thesis layer — a "Declare a thesis" strip on the cockpit where you choose a setup type, direction, and required invalidation price. The verdict engine reached a reliable baseline: all five verdict states — confirming, rejecting, weakening, invalidated, and pending — render in real browser pixels. Iterations 6 and 7 completed thesis resolution ("Played out" and "Abandon" buttons) and the remaining verdict scenarios. Iteration 8 added entry and exit mark recording with spread-at-mark and realized R. Iteration 9 made an entry-marked thesis survive a watch interruption. Iteration 10 brought thesis geometry onto the price chart. Iteration 11 added honest entry-risk advisory chips at declaration — six conditions evaluated at the moment you declare, each chip carrying its measured margin in plain language.

Iteration 12 turned Tapeology into a two-page product. A persistent navigation bar now sits above every page: Cockpit, Journal, and Studies (disabled until built — no dead links). Clicking Journal opens a filterable table of every thesis ever declared — active, resolved, expired, and abandoned — with verbatim reasons for each outcome. A real backend restart mid-QA-run confirmed the honesty: the resolved rows were byte-identical before and after the restart, and no history was lost or rewritten.

Iteration 13 completed the journal's detail half. Every row in the Journal list is now a link. Opening a thesis takes you to a full review page: the frozen expected-behaviour statements, the complete verdict timeline at true clock time with per-transition evidence, your entry and exit marks with spread-at-mark and realized R, frozen risk flags, and four machine-derived execution checks. Each check — did you enter before the tape confirmed, did you chase the entry beyond the first-confirmation price, did you hold through your own invalidation level, did you exit while the thesis was still confirming — carries an enum result (FLAGGED or CLEAN) and a plain-language explanation quoting the actual measured timestamps and prices. Relevant mistake tags are pre-selected for your upcoming review. The checks are computed once at resolution and never recomputed at read: the same defining-moment discipline that governs verdict events, risk flags, and marks. The per-statement final statuses are the one remaining clause — they will be persisted and rendered in iteration 14 alongside the first review grades and the save flow.

## What it can do today

The product lets users watch any stock ticker (simulated, historical, or live) and see a real-time cockpit identifying buyer control, seller control, bid absorption, ask absorption, and unclear tape with confidence scores. Users can replay historical sessions, stream live tickers, search for symbols, pause and resume a watch without losing state, and view a price chart with tape-state markers on true clock-time candles. Users can declare a trading thesis, watch it judged live across all five verdict states with plain-language evidence, see the declared thesis geometry drawn on the chart, mark their actual entry and exit prices verbatim, see the realized move in R units, close a thesis as played out or abandoned, survive a watch interruption with the thesis intact, and receive honest amber entry-risk chips at declaration. Users can navigate to a persistent Journal page listing all declared theses in a filterable table, and click any row to open a full detail page showing the frozen statements, the verdict timeline at true clock time with evidence, risk flags, entry/exit marks, and machine-derived execution checks with pre-selected mistake-tag suggestions.
