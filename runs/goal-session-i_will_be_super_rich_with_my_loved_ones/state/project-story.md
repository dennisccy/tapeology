# Project story so far

Tapeology is a tape-reading and trade-journaling tool that watches stock trades as they happen, tells you what the market is actually doing right now, keeps an honest restart-proof record of every thesis you have ever declared against that read, and lets you replay your setup grammar over a historical window to measure how it would have played out.

## How it has grown

The product started as a real-time tape cockpit. Type in a ticker, hit Watch, and the screen fills with live data: recent trades colour-coded by side, 14 calculated tape features, a tape-state verdict with a confidence score, and a price chart with true clock-time candles. The engine is built to be honest: high aggression with no price move reads as absorption rather than control; a weak or mixed tape reads as unclear. Symbol search, historical replay, live streaming, and pause/resume all work without tearing down the cockpit.

Early iterations built the thesis layer — a "Declare a thesis" strip on the cockpit where you choose a setup type, direction, and required invalidation price. The verdict engine reached a reliable baseline: all five verdict states render in real browser pixels. Iterations 6 through 11 completed thesis resolution, all verdict scenarios, entry and exit mark recording with spread and realized R, thesis survival across watch interruptions, geometry drawn on the price chart, and honest entry-risk advisory chips at declaration.

Iteration 12 turned Tapeology into a two-page product with a persistent navigation bar and a filterable Journal. Iteration 13 completed the journal detail page. Iteration 14 finished the review pillar: statement status badges, two grade labels (outcome and process, independently scored), and a Save Review button with mistake tags from the backend taxonomy. Iteration 15 opened the evidence layer with per-horizon excursion outcomes in R. Iteration 16 completed the evidence layer by adding an Analytics view to the Journal — honest, segregated statistics partitioned by data feed and config fingerprint, with abandonment always in the denominator and spread cost always shown.

Iteration 17 improved the tape-reading engine under the hood: rolling-feature maintenance became truly incremental, delivering an approximately 18-fold speedup on dense real-data streams. Iteration 18 used a committed real-data recording to build the Studies page — the last piece of the evidence layer before the cue layer is allowed to exist. Iteration 19 fixed the browser-QA substrate, captured every studies-honesty state in pixels, and flipped J-58 through J-62 all to passing — fully opening the Evidence-before-cues gate.

Iteration 20 crossed into the cue layer for the first time. While you hold a journaled position with an entry mark, the thesis strip now answers "does the tape still support this?" with a management stance block: Thesis Intact (emerald), Thesis Weakening (amber), or Thesis Invalidated (rose/terminal), each with a plain-language evidence sentence and live readouts of how far price is from the invalidation in dollars and in R. All three moments were captured in browser pixels on a live simulated session; the full backend suite (696 tests) was independently re-run green with no regressions. The strip is pixel-identical to before when no entry mark is present — honesty enforced at the source.

## What it can do today

The product lets users watch any stock ticker (simulated, historical, or live) and see a real-time cockpit identifying buyer control, seller control, bid and ask absorption, and unclear tape. Users can replay historical sessions, stream live tickers, search for symbols, pause and resume a watch, and view a price chart with tape-state markers at true clock time. Users can declare a trading thesis, watch it judged live across all five verdict states with plain-language evidence, mark their actual entry and exit verbatim, see the realized move in R units, and close a thesis honestly. Users can navigate to a persistent Journal, open any thesis for a full review with frozen statements, final-status badges, outcome and process grades, execution checks, and saved mistake tags. On any ended thesis, users can read per-horizon excursion outcomes in R anchored separately at confirmation and at entry. Users can switch the Journal to an Analytics view and read honest, segregated statistics of all their recorded theses. Users can navigate to the Studies page, run a deterministic replay study of a setup grammar over a chosen window, compare results side-by-side with a seeded random-time null baseline — with honesty stamps, n counts, sample caveats, and a "Descriptive only — not trading advice" label always visible — and see cancelled, failed, and partial studies explained clearly. While holding a journaled position with an entry mark, users can see at a glance whether the tape still supports their thesis, with live distance-to-invalidation in dollars and R.
