# Project story so far

Tapeology is a tape-reading tool that watches stock trades as they happen and tells you, in plain language, what the market is actually doing right now — whether buyers are in control, sellers are pressing, or the tape is unclear — and lets you declare and track your own trading thesis against that live read.

## How it has grown

The product started as a real-time tape cockpit. Type in a ticker, hit Watch, and the screen fills with live data: recent trades colour-coded by side, 14 calculated tape features, a tape-state verdict (buyer control, seller control, bid absorption, ask absorption, unclear), and a confidence score. The engine is built to be honest: high aggression with no price move reads as absorption rather than control; a weak or mixed tape reads as unclear. You can replay historical sessions, stream a live ticker, search symbols, and view a price chart with tape-state markers on true clock-time candles. Pausing and resuming work without tearing down the cockpit.

Iterations 1 and 2 built the thesis layer. Two simulation scenarios were added — a decaying buyer-control phase and an absorption-before-reversal scenario — along with a proven-inert research attachment point. Then the thesis strip arrived: while watching a ticker, a "Declare a thesis" bar appears. You fill in your trade idea — setup type, direction, and a required invalidation price — and the strip expands to show live expected-behaviour statements ("met," "not yet," "violated"), a pending verdict badge, and an honesty stamp. Bad inputs are refused immediately with a clear message; nothing is recorded on rejection. 332 backend tests were passing.

Iteration 3 cleared a build-tooling conflict that had blocked browser verification and ran the thesis declaration flow and its error-rejection matrix end-to-end in a real browser. All 13 required smoke journeys came back green. But the screenshots of the thesis strip were captured at the wrong scroll position — only the price chart frame was visible each time, with the strip just below the fold. Formally closing out the thesis-strip journeys was deferred to the next full-depth round with a stricter evidence rule.

Iteration 4 built and unit-proven the full verdict-transition engine — the component that continuously judges the tape against a declared thesis. All four setup types are implemented: an absorption-reversal only confirms when the tape actually flips and lifts price (never just because aggression is being absorbed); a level-break stays pending no matter how strong control is until price actually crosses the declared level; a trend-continuation rejects (but stays alive) when the opposite side takes control; a failed-move fade confirms while the failed push is absorbed. Confirmation requires the tape condition to hold through a configurable dwell window, so a single flickering moment never moves the verdict. A decisive print through the declared invalidation level auto-resolves the thesis immediately with the offending price recorded. The verdict timeline is append-only and served by a new journal endpoint. 21 new unit tests were added (353 total, zero regressions) and the frontend was updated to show the live verdict chip with color semantics and a plain-language evidence sentence. However, a database migration was missed: the two new schema columns exist only in the table-creation statement, not as an ALTER TABLE on the existing dev DB. Every thesis declaration returns 503 against the persistent database, so no target journey could be verified in the browser. The engine is one migration fix away from being live.

## What it can do today

The product lets users watch any stock ticker in simulated or real mode and see a live tape cockpit that identifies buyer control, seller control, bid absorption, ask absorption, and unclear tape with confidence scores. Users can replay historical sessions, stream live tickers, search for symbols, pause and resume a watch without losing state, and view a price chart with tape-state markers on true clock-time candles. Users can also declare a trade thesis on a watched ticker — choosing a setup type, direction, and invalidation price — and watch the tape judged against it with live expected-behaviour statuses. Incoherent inputs are refused with plain-language reasons. The verdict engine (live verdict chip with evidence) is complete in code but not yet visible in the running app pending a database migration fix.
