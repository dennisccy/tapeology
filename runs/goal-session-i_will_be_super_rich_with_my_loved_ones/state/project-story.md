# Project story so far

Tapeology is a tape-reading and trade-journaling tool that watches stock trades as they happen, tells you what the market is actually doing right now, and keeps an honest, restart-proof record of every thesis you have ever declared against that read.

## How it has grown

The product started as a real-time tape cockpit. Type in a ticker, hit Watch, and the screen fills with live data: recent trades colour-coded by side, 14 calculated tape features, a tape-state verdict with a confidence score, and a price chart with true clock-time candles. The engine is built to be honest: high aggression with no price move reads as absorption rather than control; a weak or mixed tape reads as unclear. Symbol search, historical replay, live streaming, and pause/resume all work without tearing down the cockpit.

Early iterations built the thesis layer — a "Declare a thesis" strip on the cockpit where you choose a setup type, direction, and required invalidation price. The verdict engine reached a reliable baseline: all five verdict states render in real browser pixels. Iterations 6 through 11 completed thesis resolution, all verdict scenarios, entry and exit mark recording with spread and realized R, thesis survival across watch interruptions, geometry drawn on the price chart, and honest entry-risk advisory chips at declaration.

Iteration 12 turned Tapeology into a two-page product with a persistent navigation bar and a filterable Journal. Iteration 13 completed the journal detail page — click any row to open a full review showing frozen expected-behaviour statements, the complete verdict timeline at true clock time, entry and exit marks, risk flags, and four machine-derived execution checks. Iteration 14 finished the review pillar: each statement shows its final status badge, two grade labels appear (outcome and process, independently scored), and the Save Review button lets you pick mistake tags from the backend taxonomy and permanently mark a thesis as reviewed.

Iteration 15 opened the evidence layer. On any ended thesis, the journal detail page now shows a "How far the tape went (R)" section with two clearly separated blocks — one anchored at the moment the tape first confirmed the idea, one anchored at the moment of actual entry — each listing maximum favourable and adverse moves per configured time horizon, the spread cost at that anchor, and an honest TRUNCATED flag where the stream ended before a horizon could resolve. No currency anywhere; theses that predate this feature say so explicitly.

Iteration 16 completed the evidence layer. The Journal page now has an Analytics view alongside the thesis table. It shows honest, segregated statistics across all your past theses: per setup type and direction, kept separate by data feed and config fingerprint so results from different configurations are never mixed. The abandonment count is always visible in every denominator; groups with fewer than five entries are labelled as having insufficient sample; figures appear only in R units with the spread cost beside every result. A text copy fix in the same iteration ensures a thesis still in progress says "not yet available" rather than the previously incorrect "predates the feature."

## What it can do today

The product lets users watch any stock ticker (simulated, historical, or live) and see a real-time cockpit identifying buyer control, seller control, bid and ask absorption, and unclear tape. Users can replay historical sessions, stream live tickers, search for symbols, pause and resume a watch, and view a price chart with tape-state markers at true clock time. Users can declare a trading thesis, watch it judged live across all five verdict states with plain-language evidence, mark their actual entry and exit verbatim, see the realized move in R units, and close a thesis honestly. Users can navigate to a persistent Journal, open any thesis for a full review with frozen statements, final-status badges, outcome and process grades, execution checks, and saved mistake tags. On any ended thesis, users can read per-horizon excursion outcomes in R anchored separately at confirmation and at entry. Users can also switch the Journal to an Analytics view and read honest, segregated statistics of all their recorded theses, partitioned by data feed and config fingerprint, with abandonment always in the denominator and spread cost always alongside the result figures.
