# Project story so far

Tapeology is a tape-reading tool that watches stock trades as they happen and tells you, in plain language, what the market is actually doing right now — whether buyers are in control, sellers are pressing, or the tape is unclear — and lets you declare and track your own trading thesis against that live read.

## How it has grown

The product started as a real-time tape cockpit. Type in a ticker, hit Watch, and the screen fills with live data: recent trades colour-coded by side, 14 calculated tape features, a tape-state verdict with a confidence score, and a price chart with true clock-time candles. The engine is built to be honest: high aggression with no price move reads as absorption rather than control; a weak or mixed tape reads as unclear. Symbol search, historical replay, live streaming, and pause/resume all work without tearing down the cockpit.

Iterations 1 and 2 built the thesis layer — a "Declare a thesis" strip that appears while watching a ticker. Fill in your trade idea (setup type, direction, and a required invalidation price) and the strip expands to show live expected-behaviour statements, a pending verdict badge, and an honesty stamp. Bad inputs are refused immediately with a plain-language message; nothing is recorded on rejection. The verdict engine was also built and fully unit-tested across all four setup types: an absorption-reversal only confirms when price actually flips; a level-break stays pending until price crosses the declared level; a trend-continuation rejects (but stays alive) while the opposite side has control; a failed-move fade confirms while the failed push is being absorbed. Confirmation requires the tape condition to hold through a dwell window so a single flickering moment never moves the verdict.

Iteration 3 cleared a tooling conflict that had blocked browser verification and confirmed the full thesis declaration flow in a real browser — but screenshots of the thesis strip were captured at the wrong scroll position each time, and the verdict engine had a hidden persistence problem.

Iteration 4 fully assembled the verdict engine and built the frontend verdict chip (colour-coded: slate for pending, emerald for confirming, amber for weakening, rose for rejecting, rose-with-ring for invalidated) along with a plain-language evidence line and the terminal invalidated treatment. The journal endpoint and verdict timeline were added. But a database upgrade was missing from the production database: every thesis declaration returned a server error, so the verdict chip was never visible in a real browser.

Iteration 5 fixed that. The database upgrade now runs automatically when the app starts — the saved journal is brought to the current schema in place, and the two previously stranded "active" theses (left by the atomicity defect) are swept to expired on startup. Thesis recording is now atomic: the thesis row and its first journal entry are saved together or not at all, so a broken declaration can never leave a half-recorded ghost blocking future ones. With the persistence layer repaired, the verdict engine ran in a real browser for the first time: declaring a thesis, watching it go from pending to confirming or rejecting, reading the inline validation message when you enter a wrong-side price, and watching the terminal invalidated badge appear with the offending print in the evidence line — all of these now work against the real running app. Four journeys moved from failing to passing this iteration; five more are within one moment-correct browser capture each.

## What it can do today

The product lets users watch any stock ticker (simulated, historical, or live) and see a real-time cockpit that identifies buyer control, seller control, bid absorption, ask absorption, and unclear tape with confidence scores. Users can replay historical sessions, stream live tickers, search for symbols, pause and resume a watch without losing state, and view a price chart with tape-state markers on true clock-time candles. Users can declare a trade thesis on a watched ticker — choosing a setup type, direction, and invalidation price — and watch the tape judged live with a colour-coded verdict badge and plain-language evidence. Incoherent inputs are refused with a clear inline message. When the tape invalidates a thesis, a terminal rose badge appears with the offending print recorded. The verdict engine delivers four of five verdict states in the browser (pending, confirming, rejecting, invalidated); the weakening state is unit-proven and one scenario capture away from being browser-verified.
