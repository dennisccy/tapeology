# Project story so far

Tapeology is a tape-reading and trade-journaling tool that watches stock trades as they happen, tells you what the market is actually doing right now, keeps an honest restart-proof record of every thesis you have ever declared against that read, and lets you replay your setup grammar over a historical window to measure how it would have played out.

## How it has grown

The product started as a real-time tape cockpit. Type in a ticker, hit Watch, and the screen fills with live data: recent trades colour-coded by side, 14 calculated tape features, a tape-state verdict with a confidence score, and a price chart with true clock-time candles. The engine is built to be honest: high aggression with no price move reads as absorption rather than control; a weak or mixed tape reads as unclear.

Early iterations built the thesis layer — a "Declare a thesis" strip where you choose a setup type, direction, and required invalidation price. All five verdict states render in real browser pixels. The review pillar followed: a persistent Journal with filterable rows, a review-detail page with frozen statements, independently scored outcome and process grades, execution checks, saved mistake tags from the backend taxonomy, and per-horizon excursion outcomes in R. Iteration 17 made the tape-reading engine truly incremental (~18x speedup on dense real-data streams). Iterations 18 and 19 delivered the Studies page: deterministic replay studies against a seeded random-time null baseline, with honesty stamps, sample caveats, and a "Descriptive only — not trading advice" label always in view.

Iterations 20 through 26 completed the full cue layer. The product gained a management stance (Thesis Intact, Weakening, or Invalidated, with live distance-to-invalidation in dollars and R); an eight-item entry checklist with live measured margins that immediately shows "NO FRESH TAPE" if the feed pauses; setup-forming hints with measured plain-language evidence and honest study citations; a live feed-basis badge (Simulated, IEX, or SIP); a product-wide copy-discipline sweep and sound-toggle that is always visible on the cockpit (off by default). Every cue-layer journey was proven in real browser pixels.

Iteration 27 was a real-data verification sweep using live Alpaca credentials. The product pulled 24,619 real Apple trades and correctly resolved nearly all of them as buys or sells. Seven journeys promoted from partial to fully proven: replaying a real historical session, three distinct honest edge-case panels, buy/sell side resolution on real data, a true-clock chart axis, the local-timezone window picker, bounded timeout errors, and the stream-closed state. Two partial legs remained: the "Couldn't connect" failure panel (the screenshot caught the cockpit after a reconnect replaced it) and a historical re-watch that took ~35s rather than the target <3s.

Iteration 28 closed both of those weekend-verifiable legs without touching a line of application code. Two distinct, md5-verified still screenshots now visibly hold the "Couldn't connect to the tape stream" failure panel in the viewport — proving the app never silently swallows a dropped connection. The decomposer formally ruled that the <3s re-watch is a soft aspiration, not a hard requirement; the product already meets the binding clause (loads within 30 seconds, never routinely times out), and the gap is documented as a known future improvement. With those closures the byte-identity regression sentinel also cleared. The only remaining gate to completion is two live-market legs that can only be captured during US trading hours: a real live-feed gap flipping to stale then recovering, and the IEX basis badge over a genuine live stream. Both are scheduled for the Monday market open — the product is effectively complete.

## What it can do today

The product lets users watch any stock ticker (simulated, historical, or live) and see a real-time cockpit labelled with its data-feed basis alongside live tape-state verdicts, confidence, quotes, recent trades with resolved buy/sell sides, 14 features, and a price chart at true clock time. An explicit "Couldn't connect" panel appears when the connection drops — no data is ever fabricated. Users can replay historical sessions against real SIP data, pick a window in their local timezone, and change replay speed without restarting. Honest panels explain market-closed, unknown-symbol, and empty-window states. Users can declare a trading thesis and watch it judged live across all five verdict states, mark actual entry and exit, see the realized move in R, and close the thesis honestly. An eight-item entry checklist with live measured margins shows an immediate freshness warning when the feed pauses. Management stance and setup-forming hints (with measured evidence and honest study citations) complete the cue layer. Every hint is logged in the Journal's Hints view. Users can browse the Journal for a full trade history with review grades, excursion outcomes, and analytics partitioned by data feed, and can run replay studies against a seeded random-time null baseline.
