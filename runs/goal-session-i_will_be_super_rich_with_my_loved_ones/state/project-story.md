# Project story so far

Tapeology is a tape-reading and trade-journaling tool that watches stock trades as they happen, tells you what the market is actually doing right now, keeps an honest restart-proof record of every thesis you have ever declared against that read, and lets you replay your setup grammar over a historical window to measure how it would have played out.

## How it has grown

The product started as a real-time tape cockpit. Type in a ticker, hit Watch, and the screen fills with live data: recent trades colour-coded by side, 14 calculated tape features, a tape-state verdict with a confidence score, and a price chart with true clock-time candles. The engine is built to be honest: high aggression with no price move reads as absorption rather than control; a weak or mixed tape reads as unclear. Symbol search, historical replay, live streaming, and pause/resume all work without tearing down the cockpit.

Early iterations built the thesis layer — a "Declare a thesis" strip on the cockpit where you choose a setup type, direction, and required invalidation price. The verdict engine reached a reliable baseline: all five verdict states render in real browser pixels. Iterations 6 through 11 completed thesis resolution, all verdict scenarios, entry and exit mark recording with spread and realized R, thesis survival across watch interruptions, geometry drawn on the price chart, and honest entry-risk advisory chips at declaration.

Iteration 12 turned Tapeology into a two-page product with a persistent navigation bar and a filterable Journal. Iterations 13 through 16 finished the review pillar — statement status badges, two grade labels (outcome and process, independently scored), a Save Review button with mistake tags from the backend taxonomy, honest segregated analytics, and per-horizon excursion outcomes in R. Iteration 17 made the tape-reading engine truly incremental (~18x speedup on dense real-data streams). Iterations 18 and 19 built and pixel-verified the Studies page: deterministic replay studies against a seeded random-time null baseline, with honesty stamps, sample caveats, and a "Descriptive only — not trading advice" label always in view. That closed the "evidence before cues" gate, allowing the cue layer to begin.

Iteration 20 shipped the first cue: while you hold a journaled position with an entry mark, the thesis strip shows a management stance — Thesis Intact, Thesis Weakening, or Thesis Invalidated — with a plain-language evidence sentence and live readouts of how far price is from the invalidation in dollars and in R.

Iteration 21 shipped the second cue: before you mark your entry, the thesis strip now shows a full entry checklist. Eight named checks each display their live measured margin — whether the verdict is confirming, whether enough events have arrived, whether the feed is live, whether the data is arriving on schedule, whether the spread is stable, whether trades are arriving fast enough, whether the entry price leaves enough room to the invalidation, and whether price has moved too far since the setup triggered. An overall stance (emerald for "conditions met", slate for "not met", rose for "tape against", amber for "no fresh tape") publishes through its own dwell to avoid flapping. A "nearest to flipping" line tells you which condition is closest to changing. No numbers are derived on the page — everything comes verbatim from the server. A known gap (the served checklist goes stale when the feed is paused) is J-64's work for iteration 22.

## What it can do today

The product lets users watch any stock ticker (simulated, historical, or live) and see a real-time cockpit identifying buyer control, seller control, bid and ask absorption, and unclear tape. Users can replay historical sessions, stream live tickers, search for symbols, pause and resume a watch, and view a price chart with tape-state markers at true clock time. Users can declare a trading thesis, watch it judged live across all five verdict states with plain-language evidence, mark their actual entry and exit verbatim, see the realized move in R units, and close a thesis honestly. Users can navigate to a persistent Journal, open any thesis for a full review with frozen statements, final-status badges, outcome and process grades, execution checks, and saved mistake tags. On any ended thesis, users can read per-horizon excursion outcomes in R anchored separately at confirmation and at entry. Users can switch the Journal to an Analytics view for honest, segregated statistics partitioned by data feed and config fingerprint. Users can navigate to the Studies page, run a deterministic replay study of a setup grammar over a chosen window, and compare results side-by-side with a seeded random-time null baseline. Before marking entry on an active thesis, users see an eight-item entry checklist with live measured margins, an overall stance chip, and a nearest-counterevidence line. While holding a journaled position, users see at a glance whether the tape still supports their thesis, with live distance-to-invalidation in dollars and R.
