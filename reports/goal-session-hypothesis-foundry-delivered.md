## How it came together

The chapter opened by formally retiring the previous research chapter and turning off its self-continuing idea loop, so this new chapter could run as a bounded, one-time pass rather than an open-ended search.

Next, the team built the actual research desk section for this chapter and wrote down the rulebook that decides what counts as a scientifically complete, testable idea versus one that still needs a human judgment call. They then displayed the chapter's real starting numbers on screen, so anyone could see day one honestly, before any testing began.

After that, the team proved the underlying testing machinery was trustworthy by running it against a set of practice examples, including checking that it correctly reverses its answer when a bet's direction is flipped — a basic sanity check that the machine wasn't fooling itself.

The centerpiece came next: the one real, permanently-recorded research decision for this chapter. All eleven eligible ideas were checked against the rulebook. None of them were ready to test — every single one was held back for a clearly stated reason, and that decision was locked in before anyone could see what a test might have shown, so there was no chance of quietly cherry-picking a favorite.

The team then ran the one real evaluation pass over that locked-in, empty plan, and proved — with a timestamp and independently verified data fingerprints — that the pass genuinely happened and touched none of the chapter's protected research data. Because there was nothing left ready to test, the honest outcome was "nothing to evaluate" — and that is exactly what the rulebook says is an acceptable way for this chapter to end; the plan for this chapter always allowed for a result of zero surviving ideas and no market outcome ever being read at all. Checking this closely surfaced two small honesty problems, both of which were disclosed rather than hidden: one summary count could be worked out two different, disagreeing ways in two different places, and simply opening the results screen was quietly touching a small internal coordination file behind the scenes, something it was only supposed to read from, not write to.

The first problem was fixed properly: the count now has a single official place it is calculated, with a permanent check that guarantees nothing else can silently disagree with it. The second problem — the results screen quietly touching that small coordination file every time it is opened — could not be safely fixed inside this locked-down chapter without risking the integrity of everything already recorded, so it was left open on purpose. The project owner reviewed both issues directly and ruled that neither one puts any of this chapter's real findings at risk: nothing scientific was ever miscounted, no protected data was ever touched, and both issues are now on record as known, accepted loose ends for a future round of work to close properly. It's also worth being upfront that a first, discarded attempt at generating this chapter's research plan really did happen before the version that was locked in and made official — it was caught and corrected before anything was finalized, and the owner has reviewed and accepted that history rather than having it erased.

The closing stretch added the final summary screen described above, then did one last full check of everything — every screen, the complete automated test suite, and every locked file's integrity — with the product completely unchanged from the round before. Everything still held. Along the way, one recorded walkthrough video from an earlier round did not capture properly, and one saved screenshot came out blank — in both cases the same evidence was available and double-checked through another screen, so nothing was actually lost, just imperfectly filed. A couple of small record-keeping notes in an earlier written report also turned out to be slightly wrong (they named files that weren't actually touched). None of this was quietly cleaned up or rewritten to look tidier afterward — it is recorded here exactly as it happened.
