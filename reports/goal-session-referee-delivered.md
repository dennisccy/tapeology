# Delivered — The Referee (Tapeology Era 6)

**Session:** referee
**Date:** 2026-08-16
**Final verdict:** GOAL_ACHIEVED
**Iterations:** 15

## What you can do today

Tapeology still does everything it always did. Watch the live tape update on the Cockpit. Look up a stock's price map on the Structure page. Scan for chart setups on the Desk.

This chapter adds a new area to the Desk page. It has three parts: Referee Registry, Referee Adjudications, and Referee Runs. Together they teach the product to check its own homework before it ever calls a chart pattern a real edge.

With it, you can:

- Open Referee Registry to see a shortlist of candidate research questions, each with a plain-English reason and live evidence counts.
- Pick a question and register it for real. The system locks in today's date as its honest starting line. Nothing recorded before that date can ever count as proof afterward.
- See exactly how much evidence stands behind each research question. If there isn't enough evidence yet, the page says so honestly instead of guessing.
- See how long a question still has to wait — in plain calendar days, and in true recorded trading sessions, since not every day brings fresh evidence.
- Open Referee Adjudications to see a plain verdict for every registered question, plus the full evidence trail behind it.
- Open Referee Runs to start a check, watch it run, cancel it, or look back at every check that's ever been run.
- Trust that the core trading strategy is protected. It can never be swapped for a new one unless that new strategy has passed a genuine, matching check — there is no way around it.
- Ask the Claude assistant to read the same research data for you, the same way it already reads everything else in the app.

One honest note: nobody has registered a real research question against the product's actual trading history yet. That step is still up to a person, on purpose. The system is built, tested, and ready. It just hasn't been pointed at a real question yet.

## How it came together

The team started with an honest baseline. Every existing screen — the live tape, the price map, the chart-setup scanner — was checked and confirmed still working. The new fact-checking system was confirmed to not exist yet, so nothing later would look better than it really was.

Work then began quietly behind the scenes. The product learned to count how much trading evidence it already had. It then learned to describe every signal and every test trade the same way, as one shared, detailed record instead of two different ones.

Next came the actual judge: a statistics engine built to tell a real pattern from chance. Early on, the team double-checked the engine's own work instead of trusting the build report, and found a real flaw — in one narrow case, it could report more confidence than the math actually allowed. They fixed it, then proved the fix held, before building anything else on top of it.

The team then built a fair-comparison tool. For every recorded signal, it now picks believable "nothing happened" moments from the same stock, matched by time of day, and measures them the exact same way as the real thing. That is how a real pattern gets told apart from a lucky coincidence.

A permanent notebook came next. A research question can now be written down before its answer exists, with an honest starting date that locks in automatically. While reviewing this feature, the team found and closed a real loophole: the starting date could quietly have been set to an earlier day, letting old evidence sneak in and count as fresh proof.

With the notebook in place, the team built the judging step itself: comparing a written-down question against its fair comparisons, and producing one honest, permanent verdict. No later re-check can ever change it.

The first Referee screen then appeared on the Desk page. A person could open "Referee Registry," see candidate research questions with live evidence counts, choose one, and register it for real — the first fact-checking action anyone could actually perform.

Right after that, the team protected the product's most important asset. The core trading strategy can no longer be swapped for a new one unless a genuine, matching certificate backs the change, with no hidden way around the rule.

Two more screens then completed the Desk-page picture: "Referee Adjudications," showing each question's plain verdict and its evidence trail, and "Referee Runs," where a person can start, watch, cancel, or review a check. The Claude assistant connector grew to reach the same data.

A clean-up round followed. It replaced shortcuts and placeholder results from earlier, rushed rounds with real, freshly-run proof, and the team declared the chapter finished for the first time.

A further round made the wait estimate honest. Instead of only counting raw calendar days, the Registry screen also shows how many trading sessions are truly on record, and how long the longest quiet stretch has been. The chapter closed cleanly a second time.

One more gap then surfaced. The amount of evidence behind each research family — chart patterns, and backtested trades — had always been tracked, but never shown on screen. A round added two new panels to the Registry page, making that evidence, and an honest warning when the strategy side isn't ready to be judged yet, visible for the first time.

In the final round, the team tied off the last two loose ends without changing the product at all. Two checks that had been skipped earlier for time were run for real. A screenshot that had been cut off partway down a long page was retaken in full. With every promise now backed by fresh, checked proof, the team closed the chapter, and a second, independent review agreed.

True to the system's own purpose, the chapter ends without a single confirmed discovery. The finished judge has not yet called any real chart pattern a proven edge. That is not a shortfall. It is the whole point. A judge willing to say "not enough evidence yet," instead of manufacturing a result from too little data, is exactly what this chapter set out to build.
