## What you can do today

On the Desk page you can now see the full honest story of how Tapeology tests new trading ideas, from first spark to final verdict — and a Claude conversation can read all of it too.

You can see exactly how much real market data is on hand and which research checks it can and can't support yet, with a clear warning label on any count that needs care. You can watch buying and selling pressure tracked tick by tick against chart signals, without the system ever peeking at data from the future. Every trading idea the system tries is permanently recorded — including the ones that fail — so nothing gets quietly swept away. Ideas are checked the way a real trader would experience them, walking forward through time rather than testing on data they've already seen. New market data gets recorded and locked away safely until it's the right moment to look at it. A Graduation view shows exactly which ideas reached which stage on their way toward being taken seriously, including a permanent record of any that failed for good. A newer Feature Snapshots view shows which datasets have actually been prepared for research, with honest counts of anything left out because it was sealed away or gone stale. And three real research questions about market behavior were run all the way through, with honest answers reported even when the answer was "no effect found."

## How it came together

The project started by building an honest ledger of exactly what market data it had on hand — no pretending to have more evidence than it did — and a careful "one pass, no peeking" way of reading that data to measure buying and selling pressure.

From there it connected those pressure readings to the chart patterns the product already knew how to spot, always checking that nothing was allowed to see into the future. It then built a fast, cheap way to try out many trading ideas at once and record every single trial — wins and losses alike — so no idea's history could ever quietly disappear.

Next came a validation engine that checks surviving ideas the way a real trader lives through time: walking forward day by day rather than testing against data it had already seen. A recording-and-vault system started bringing in fresh real market data and sealing it safely away until the right moment to examine it. A Graduation stage then tracked which ideas earned the right to move forward, and which failed for good — with nothing hidden along the way.

All of this was surfaced on the Desk page and made readable through a Claude conversation, and three real, pre-agreed research questions were run through the whole pipeline, each one answered honestly.

A later stretch of work focused purely on trustworthiness: a misleading data count got a clear warning label, two very slow safety checks were sped up from tens of minutes down to seconds, and a handful of small open questions were escalated to the project owner for a direct ruling rather than guessed at. Everything was then re-verified from scratch, and nothing was found to have gone wrong.

Even after the project believed it was finished, it kept finding one more genuinely useful gap to close. It added the Graduation view to the Desk page, complete with a real screenshot proving an idea that failed for good. Then it noticed datasets were being prepared behind the scenes with no way to see which ones were actually ready — so it added the Feature Snapshots view, with honest counts of anything excluded.
